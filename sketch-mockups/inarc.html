Inarc — the menu-bar mark

Two bottom-open arcs of remaining quota, a 50% tick at top dead centre on each, the agents docked as beads in the mouth the arcs leave open, a creature in the middle that only moves when work is in flight, chevrons when something is waiting on you, and a sleeping pet when the window is gone. Drawn to 22pt. 1pt = 1px throughout.

1  Construction — ≈15×, every number real

The radial budget is the whole design. Sixteen points of ink give eight points of radius, and it is spent like this: 1.50 outer stroke, 1.80 counter, 1.20 inner stroke, 1.20–1.61 counter, then whatever is left is the animal — a body 3.80 × 4.80. That is four parallel elements across the mark, which is the measured ceiling. Nothing else fits, which is why the beads are not below the ring: they are in the mouth, on the track at R 6.90, costing zero extra height.

Honest timings: idle 0–2.0s · wake + first bead 2.0–2.4s · mouth opens 96°→138° and beads 2–5 land 2.8–5.6s · five running 5.6–8.0s · one exits 8.0–8.8s · morph to chevrons 8.8–9.2s · held 9.2–12.3s · cleared 12.3–12.9s · 5-h drains 12.9–15.4s (amber under 15%) · asleep 15.8–17.4s · window resets 17.6s.

Two compressions I did not get for free. CSS cannot tween path data, so the mouth is not continuous: it cross-fades between a 104° track, a 138° track and a 126° sleep track over 400ms, which reads as the mouth opening but is really a dissolve. And the beads keep one radius (1.05) through the whole loop instead of stepping 1.10→1.00 at four agents — the gallery shows the true sizes. Everything else is the real geometry: the beads really do slide along R 6.90 by rotating about the ring centre, and the 5-hour arc really is a stroke-dasharray on the full track with pathLength="100", so its head crosses the tick at exactly 50%.

Pixel parity

The ring is centred at (12, 11) with an 8.00 outer radius, so the silhouette’s cardinal edges land on whole device pixels at 1× — top at y=3.00, bottom at y=19.00. The consequence is that the outer stroke’s inner edge falls on a half pixel (4.50 / 17.50), so the ring’s inner boundary is a 50% row at the cardinal points. I chose the outline over the counter: the outline is the silhouette. A circle is off-grid everywhere except those four points anyway, which is the honest reason the design leans on arc length and angular position rather than on crisp edges.

What I gave up

Countable agents past four. Five beads touch; six or more become a bar. The dock is 10.36pt of arc and a legible bead with a legible gap costs 3.4pt of it. Everything else in this design was affordable; this was not.
Exact blocked counts. The morph says ‘one’ (1.50pt ring) or ‘more than one’ (2.30pt ring, double-beat). A number cannot live inside a 13pt circle that already holds three chevrons.
The ticks moved outward. The sketch puts them just inside each arc. Inside each arc is where the next element already lives — the counters are 1.80 and 1.20 and cannot host a 1.00pt mark with clearance on both sides. Outward, the outer pip has infinite room and only the inner pip pays, once, at 12 o’clock.
Bead status colour is colour-only. A hollow bead needs a stroke plus a counter inside 2.00pt: impossible. So blocked is never carried by a bead — it is carried by the whole-mark morph, which is exactly what the sketch already said.

Why a breathing mouth is safe

The mouth opens from 96° to 138° as agents arrive, which changes how long each track is. That would normally be fatal for a gauge — the same 72% would sit at a different angle. It is safe here because the track is symmetric about 12 o’clock and each arc fills from one end, so the halfway point of the track is top dead centre at every mouth width. The one calibrated reading the user asked for — the tick is half — is invariant. Everything else is a glance.
