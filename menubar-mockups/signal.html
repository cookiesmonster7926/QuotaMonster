What each mark means

Shell + fill5-hour window remaining. Full = untouched, empty = spent. Drains right‑to‑left exactly like the system battery.
Thin tick7-day window remaining, on the same 0–100% axis. Sits ahead of the fill when the week is the looser constraint.
ShadowWhat the current burn rate will eat in the next 30 minutes. No shadow = nothing is running. Floor of 3 pt so one agent is never invisible.
Front lineWhere you will be in 30 minutes. The crisp boundary makes the shadow readable over any wallpaper.
Red sliverThe only colour in the design. Lit when the projection exhausts the window before it resets. The low‑fuel lamp, nothing else.
NumberTime to the next thing that changes your situation: exhaustion if it comes first (red lit), otherwise the reset (red dark). Wait time when blocked.
GhostWhere the front stood before an agent finished. Fades over 1.1 s and is gone. The only transient mark.
Solid slabA human is blocking work. The whole object inverts; slots carry both levels through. Persists, without motion, until it clears.

The integration, stated plainly

Quota and activity are the same channel because activity is the derivative of quota. The shadow is not a second gauge bolted on; it is the same fill, marked as already-committed. That is the honest reason they can share a form, and it is the thing the previous fifteen never had.

The transient is a change to marks that already exist — the front retreats, the shadow shortens, a bite closes, a ghost of the old front fades. Nothing new appears and nothing has to be dismissed. After a fan-out of seven drains to zero you get a two-second staircase, the lamp releasing, one last ghost dissolving, and then a plain still battery at a visibly lower level. The event is legible and it leaves a correct reading behind.

Urgency is a state of the whole object, not a badge and not a hue. Inverting to a solid slab is the largest possible change a 28 × 14 pt monochrome mark can make, it works identically on a light bar, a dark bar and a busy wallpaper, and it costs nothing from the colour budget — which is spent entirely on one red sliver meaning one thing.

Where the borrowed language breaks, and I would rather say it than hide it: a battery recharges when you plug it in, and a quota window refills on a wall clock whether you are working or not. So the reset is time-based, not effort-based — which is why the number carries the clock and the fill never “charges”. And two windows on one 0–100% axis is the one part of this that is learned rather than known; the 7-day mark stays quiet until it is the binding wall, and then it cuts the fill so you cannot miss where the real limit is.
