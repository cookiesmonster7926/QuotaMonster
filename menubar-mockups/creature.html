QuotaMonster — the menu-bar creature

One 62pt creature. Its body fill is the 5-hour window, the ground it stands on is the 7-day window, the quills on its back are the agents running right now, and a quill detaching and rising is an agent finishing. When a session needs you, the creature stops being a side view and turns to face you. Quills are the rate; the fill is the integral of that rate — the same fact drawn twice, honestly.

Content box 22pt tall inside a 33pt bar. Item width 62pt, constant in every state — about 29% of the measured 216pt ceiling.

Fixed 62pt, always. Nothing in the glyph changes the item’s width — the fill, the ground, the quills and the overrun ticks all live inside a fixed box, so no neighbouring icon ever jumps. 62pt is ~29% of the measured 216pt ceiling, which leaves room for the ceiling to shrink as more menu-bar apps are added. Everything is a template image driven by one ink colour, so it inverts correctly on a light bar, a dark bar and over any wallpaper.

Precedence, one rule: blocked > everything. Any other combination composes freely, because each signal owns a different physical property — fill, ground, quills, posture — and they cannot collide. Blocked is the only state that seizes the whole silhouette, and it still keeps the 5h fill and the 7d ground readable rather than blanking them.

The blink is the only thing with a clock on it. While blocked, the eyes blink once every 60s (160ms) and stop after 10 minutes — by then the wait number has grown from 2m to 12m and the creature has leaned 1pt toward the clock, and a slowly-changing static form beats a repeating twitch for a user who sees this four hundred times a week. The demo blink is compressed to one per loop so you can see it. Everything else here is triggered by an event and terminates: nothing animates because time passed.

Why this integrates instead of stacking badges

Quills and fill are the same fact at two time-scales. The quills are how many agents are running; the pale ghost ahead of the solid fill is where the 5h window lands one hour from now at the current burn. Rate and integral, drawn on one body. They are kept separate on purpose: ten cheap agents can burn less than two Opus sessions with fat contexts, so agent count is not burn rate.
The completion event transforms an existing mark. Nothing new appears when an agent finishes — its own quill flicks, detaches and rises away. After a fan-out of seven drains to zero you get a brief flurry of quills leaving, one settle, the eye shutting, and then stillness. If you miss the moment, the state it announced is still readable: fewer quills, or a closed eye. That is the honest answer to putting a transient in a persistent icon — the moment is the derivative of a state you can still read afterwards.
Urgency changes the object’s state, not its contents. A side view becomes a front view. The silhouette changes category, which is the loudest thing a 22pt mark can do without moving or shouting in colour. The 5h fill and the 7d ground survive the morph.
The colour budget is one condition, and the eye count is the escalation. Amber appears only as the creature’s eye — one lens for one blocked session, a pair for two, a triangle of three for three or more — and only when a human is blocking work. Never for high quota, never for overrun, never for staleness: those are carried by weight, by ticks and by a dashed outline. The previous fifteen variants all turned amber for everything; here amber has exactly one meaning, the total amber area is under 5pt², and the morph already did the work before you saw the colour.

Motion budget — every animation, and when it stops

quill raise 160ms ease-out, 40ms stagger · on agent start · terminates
quill release 700ms (120ms detach flick + 580ms rise & fade) · on agent finish · terminates
spine re-space 180ms · after any quill-count change · terminates
settle 260ms squash-release · once, only when the count reaches zero · terminates
turn to face 220ms · once per block event · terminates
turn back 200ms · once when the block clears · terminates
blink 160ms · once per 60s while blocked · hard stop at 10 minutes
fill / ghost 400ms ease · only on a change of ≥1% · terminates
Total idle motion: zero. There is no animation whose trigger is “time passed” except the blink, and the blink has a cap. A quiet machine gives you a completely still menu bar.

Where it is weak

The two-eye state is the closest thing here to cute, and I would rather say so plainly than pretend otherwise: two round eyes facing you at 22pt sits one step from a smiley. The common case — one blocked session — is deliberately a single lens, which reads as an aperture rather than a face, and the three-eye state is unsettling rather than sweet. It is only the middle rung that wobbles; the fix is to offset the pair vertically so it stops being symmetrical, which costs nothing.
The 7-day window gets the least resolution. You can see the ground shortening over a week and you can see when it binds, but you cannot read 18% off it. That is deliberate — the week is almost never the question — but it is a real cost, and the exact number lives in the popover.
The 5h fill is coarse. 28% and 34% look the same; the number is doing the precision work. The shape is for the peripheral glance.
The number changes meaning in exactly one state — percent used normally, oldest wait time while blocked. Justified by how different the creature looks in that state, but it is a genuine ambiguity if someone glances at only the digits.
Quills saturate at six. Twelve agents and eight agents both read “bristling”. Beyond six concurrent agents the difference is not actionable from a menu bar, but the count is exact in the popover.
