KERF — one field, many cells

The icon is a strip of material being cut. The material is your quota, the saw teeth standing on it are the agents running right now, and every other signal is something that happens to that one object — not a badge bolted beside it. 72 × 22 pt, fixed width, template-monochrome except for one colour that is spent on exactly one state.

2 · Anatomy — built up one layer at a time (4×)

The corridor. A 36 × 8 pt frame. This is the whole field: everything else lives inside or stands on it. At rest it is a clean rectangle — the icon never looks broken or empty.

The spine — binding window, used. Fills from the left. It always shows whichever window will bite first; the 2-character tag says which one, and the digits are that window's number. 5H at 28%.

The shelf — the other window. A 2.3 pt foot filling from the far end. Subordinate by construction: a foot, not a wall. 7D at 18%. When 7D becomes the binding constraint the two swap roles — the spine becomes 7D, the shelf becomes 5H, the tag flips. Same field, no second gauge.

The reserved zone — burn × time to reset. The quota your running agents have already spoken for before the window resets. This is where activity and quota honestly share one channel: agents are the rate at which quota is consumed, so the zone's length is activity expressed in quota units. Clamped at the wall → the inward chevron lights: you run out before reset.

The crest — one tooth per running agent. Teeth stand on the bar at the frontier, on the material about to be cut. The carriage (the 2 pt block straddling the bar) means a session is working; the teeth say how hard. 0 teeth and a carriage = busy with no subagents. No carriage at all = nothing is running.

Needs input — the object changes state. The teeth retract into the caret: the thing that was cutting now points at you. The material clamps under a hold hatch (quota stays readable through it, because nothing is burning while you are the bottleneck). Colour appears here and nowhere else in the design.

An agent finished — a cell extinguishes. The tooth flares 120 ms, collapses 380 ms, and leaves a 1 pt kerf mark in its slot. The mark decays over 6 s and only then does the crest re-pack. Glance 2 s after a fan-out drains and you read a comb of fading ticks: seven things just finished here.

3 · State gallery — every state the icon can be in

Idle — nothing running

A clean two-tone rectangle. No carriage, no crest, no reserved zone. Zero agents is a real state, not an empty one — the quota reading is fully intact.

1 agent

One tooth, 3.4 pt pitch, and the carriage appears under it. Counts of 1–4 are read without counting.

5 agents

Pitch tightens to 2.9 pt from the fifth tooth. Still discrete, now reads as a handful. The reserved zone has grown with the burn, because the burn is the agents.

12 agents (and 15, and 40)

At 10+ the pitch drops to 2.2 pt, the teeth touch, the crest steps 1 pt taller: it stops being countable and becomes density. Marks clamp at ten and never overflow the field. The exact number lives in the popover.

5H high — 85% used

The spine has eaten the corridor. The crest has no room ahead of the frontier so it clamps back over the cut material — the saw still runs, there is just nothing left to cut. Chevron lit.

7D is now the binding window

Roles swap: the spine is the 7-day window, the shelf is the 5-hour one, tag and digits follow. One field, one meaning at a time — you always read the constraint that bites first.

Needs input — tier 1 (<60 s)

The whole object changes state. Teeth retract into the caret; the material clamps under a hold hatch. Three 500 ms knocks on entry, then it stops forever and holds static.

Needs input — 3 blocked, >60 s

Escalation is mass, not motion: the caret grows 22%, a hold rule appears under the entire icon, and one caret per blocked session (max 3). Nothing moves. It simply will not stop looking different.

An agent finished — t+0.4 s

Two teeth gone, two kerf marks left in their slots. The gap in the comb is the receipt. The crest does not re-pack until the marks have decayed.

Fan-out of 7 drained — t+0.8 s

The two seconds after. Seven teeth collapsed right-to-left in 400 ms; seven ticks remain and fade over 6 s. The carriage is still there because the session is still alive. Then: clean rectangle.

Data stale (>10 min)

Frame goes dashed, whole icon to 48%. The drawing is marked provisional without spending colour or motion. At the real 3-minute freshness the icon is not stale and shows nothing.

Monochrome proof — colour stripped

The same needs-input state with the accent forced to the ink colour. The morph alone carries it. Colour is reinforcement, never the signal — which is the specific failure of the fifteen rejected variants.

Needs input on a light bar

The caret reads as a dark ember against black ink; the hatch and the hold rule do the work regardless.

12 agents on a light bar

The crest, the carriage and the clamped reserved zone all survive inversion. Template-monochrome throughout.

4 · Live scenario — 20 s loop, real durations

Idle → work starts → fan-out to seven → one finishes (transient) → a session blocks on you (morph) → you answer → the fan-out drains. The frontier itself is deliberately still: at 92%/h it advances 0.5 pt per minute, which is nothing in 20 s. What moves is the reserved zone, because that is the burn — and the burn is the agents.

5 · The integration — which signals share a channel, and why they honestly can

SignalChannelThe honest reason it can share
5-hour windowThe spine — fills from the leftThe spine is always the window that will bite first. The 2-character tag names it and the digits are its number, so there is never an unlabelled percentage.
7-day windowThe shelf — a 2.3 pt foot from the far endIt is normally not the constraint, so it gets a subordinate form. When it becomes the constraint the two swap roles rather than both shouting. One field, one meaning at a time.
Live activity — is it workingThe carriage (2 pt block straddling the frontier)Presence/absence. It is the head of the saw: work is happening at the frontier, so the mark for “working” belongs at the frontier and nowhere else.
Live activity — how hardThe crest — one tooth per agentDiscrete cells: countable to 4, a handful to 9, pure density at 10+. This is the only encoding that is correct at both ends of 1–15.
Activity × quota togetherThe reserved zoneThe load-bearing integration. burn × time-to-reset. Activity is the rate at which quota is consumed, so the agents’ cost is drawn in quota units inside the quota field. Ten agents make the zone long; zero agents make it vanish. Quota and activity are not two gauges, they are cause and effect of one length.
Exhaustion before resetChevron at the wallIt is the reserved zone clamping — not a new mark, a state the zone enters when the projection runs past the wall. This is the 47-minutes-vs-2h12m warning, drawn structurally, with no colour.
Agent finished (transient)A tooth extinguishing → kerf markYou get the transient free from the structure: the completion is the removal of a cell that was already there, and the hole it leaves is the receipt. Nothing new appears.
Needs input (urgent)The whole object changes stateTeeth retract into the caret — a transformation of an existing mark, not an added badge. Hatch + silhouette + one colour, three redundant carriers. Quota stays readable through the hatch because nothing is burning while you are the bottleneck.

6 · Motion budget — every animation, its duration, its termination rule

MotionDurationTermination rule
Tooth strike (spawn)260 msOne-shot per spawn. Overshoot to 1.18, settle to 1.0. Ends.

Kerf-mark decay6 s (3 s in the compressed loop)Linear to zero opacity, then the element is gone. Never repeats.

Nothing in this design loops. Every animation is triggered by a real event and terminates. In a week of use the icon moves exactly as often as something actually happens — which for four sessions and ten-agent fan-outs is plenty, and which is why it never becomes wallpaper.

7 · Colour budget

The icon is a macOS template image — pure ink, auto-inverting — in every state except one. Colour appears only on the needs-input caret and its hold rule. There is no amber quota warning, no green “running”, no red “exhausted”: quota pressure is drawn with length and mass, exhaustion with a chevron, staleness with a dashed stroke. The monochrome-proof tile in the gallery is the test — strip the colour and needs-input is still the only state with a hatch, a caret and a rule under it.

8 · Known weaknesses

  • Only one window carries a number at a time. You read the binding window exactly and know the other is not worse; you cannot read “7D 18%” from the bar without opening the popover. This was a deliberate trade against the rejected two-line-of-digits variant, but it is a real loss.
  • 72 pt is on the wide side. Stats’ CPU module with a chart is 40–60 pt. It is well under the ~216 pt cliff and fixed-width, but a user with twenty menu-bar items pays for it. A 46 pt digits-off mode (tag + corridor only) should be a preference; it loses the exact percentage and keeps every other signal.
  • The crest clamps back over cut material above ~80% spent. When the frontier is near the wall the teeth no longer sit exactly on the frontier. It stays legible and countable, but the “the saw is where the cutting is” story breaks down precisely when quota is tightest.
  • The reserved zone is a projection, and projections lie. A 92%/h instantaneous burn extrapolated over 2h12m is aggressive; a fan-out that ends in 40 seconds makes the zone briefly wrong. It should be smoothed over ~90 s of burn history, and the chevron should require the shortfall to exceed 20 minutes before it lights, or it will habituate.
  • Teeth above ten are a lie by omission. Twelve, fifteen and forty all render as the same saturated comb. That is the correct pre-attentive answer (“a lot”) but it means the icon cannot distinguish a healthy fan-out from a runaway one. The popover must.
  • At 2.2 pt pitch the dense crest needs a Retina display. On a 1× external monitor the 10+ tier degrades to a grey smear — still readable as density, no longer readable as marks. The 1–9 tiers are safe at 1×.
  • Three carets is the ceiling for blocked sessions. Four or more blocked sessions render identically to three. With four concurrent sessions that ceiling is exactly at the edge of this user’s real workload.
  • The hatch reduces quota contrast while blocked. Readable, but measurably worse. Justified only by the claim that quota is not the actionable signal in that moment.
