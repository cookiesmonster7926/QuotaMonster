PARALLAX — a menu-bar quota mark

Two open arcs read remaining quota. A creature sits in the well. The bottom opening carries the agent tally. When something is blocked the whole mark turns amber and the outer ring divides into equal parts. One rule governs every count: a ring cut into N equal pieces. Ink is 16.00 × 16.00pt in every state; the item box is 22 × 22pt and never moves.

What it sacrifices: both counters. 1.60pt and 1.30pt are under the 2.0pt 1× counter floor, so at 1× the two arcs fuse into a banded double-line and the creature's crown touches the inner arc. I bought a 4.10pt-wide creature and a 1.50pt outer stroke with that money. The alternative — 2.0pt counters — leaves a 1.2pt creature, which is a speck.

2  Rounding audit — correction 1

Every vertex in the mark, and the radius applied to it. There is no zero-radius vertex and no cusp. Two radii sit below the ~0.75pt threshold at which a curve resolves as a curve on a 1× display: the crown valley (0.600) and the chevron caps (0.625). Neither becomes a point — they become slightly blunter curves. The complaint being answered was a sharp V between two peaks; the V is now a 0.600pt fillet only 0.458pt deep, so even when the fillet is unresolvable the crown reads as one dome, never as a spike.

The two 50% marks at 16×

the band unrolled, so the tangency can be read against a straight edge

Why a circle and not a notch. A notch is a counter, so it is governed by the 2.0pt floor. On the outer arc a 2.0pt gap subtends 2.0 / 7.25 = 15.81°; on the inner arc 27.61°. Those are not ticks, they are breaks — wider than the alert ring's own segmentation gaps, and on a remaining-quota arc a break is ambiguous with the arc simply ending there. A notch that stays under 6° measures 0.76pt and closes up at 1×. The notch cannot be made to work, so I took the circle.

How the circle avoids being a counter. The disc is not punched through and it is not oversized. Its diameter equals the band width exactly — 1.50pt on the outer arc, 1.40pt on the inner — and it is centred on the arc centreline, so it is tangent to both band edges and protrudes in neither direction. Its visibility comes from an alpha step, not from geometry: the disc is drawn in the track layer at full ink while the track itself is at 0.32, a step of 0.68 across a 1.5pt filled mass, which is well above any floor. When remaining is above 50% the live arc passes over the disc and subsumes it; the arc reaching past top dead centre is the above-half reading. The disc appearing is the below-half signal. At exactly 50% the arc's round cap lands concentric with the disc and the terminus reads as a single dot.

Read in true geometry: the band is unrolled above so tangency can be checked against a straight edge. On the real circle the band curves away from the disc's flanks, so the disc touches r 8.00 at exactly one point and r 6.50 at exactly one point and lies strictly inside the band everywhere else. That is stricter than tangency, not looser.

Eleven states. The item box is 22 × 22pt in all eleven and the ink box is 16.00 × 16.00pt in all eleven. Nothing the agent count does reaches outside the silhouette, so no neighbouring menu-bar item ever moves. Shown at 3.45× against a shared ruler that marks the item box and the ink box.

What holds the bottom edge. The tally rail has a dim track behind it exactly as the two arcs do, present in every gauge state including idle. That track runs to y 19.00, so the ink box closes at 16.00pt tall whether or not any agent is running, and a depleted tally never reads as a rendering fault. In the alert states the closed or segmented ring reaches the same edge. The segmentation phase is chosen for this: for even N the segments are centred on top and bottom dead centre, for odd N the gaps are centred on top dead centre — which puts bottom dead centre at least 25° from the nearest gap at every N ≤ 7. Both poles carry ink at every count.

8  One language, two counts

The rule

A ring is cut into N equal pieces, separated by a 2.00pt gap. Needs-input cuts the whole 360° outer ring. The agent tally cuts the 100° opening that same outer ring already leaves at the bottom. Same circle, same radius 7.25, same 1.50pt stroke, same round caps, same gap. Nothing is bolted on.

The alert ceiling, derived. A 2.00pt ink gap at r 7.25 needs a 27.936° centreline separation, because the chord is 2R sin(Δ/2) and 14.5 sin(13.968°) = 3.500pt, less 0.75pt of cap at each end. A segment stops reading as a segment and starts reading as a dash below about 2.5× the gap, i.e. 5.0pt of ink. At N=6 the segment is 5.56pt — the last count that clears it. N=7 gives 4.55pt, which is a dash. So the ring holds at six and the exact number above six goes to the menu. Honest caveat: five and six already require deliberate counting rather than a glance; one, two and three are instant.

Can the creature carry the count? No.

The method asked for the count to live in the creature's body. I built it and it fails, and the arithmetic says why: the creature is 4.10 × 3.35pt. Spreading fifteen steps across its available height gives 0.22pt per agent — a fifth of the stroke floor, five times finer than a 2× device pixel. Size, height and rhythm are all continuous magnitudes at a scale where the whole creature is four pixels tall at 1×. It can say something is happening. It cannot say five.

So the creature keeps the job it can do, and does it as a genuine part of the code rather than decoration:

CreatureMeans

The minimum addition, and why it is the tally. It stays inside the silhouette, it uses correction 3's own language, and it costs nothing in width. It is exact to three. At 1× the three-unit gaps close and the tally fuses, so the creature's posture disambiguates: a fused bar with an upright creature is three, a fused bar with a leaning creature is four or more. The two signals are redundant by design, which is the only reason the count survives 1× at all.

Stated plainly: 5, 10 and 15 agents render as the identical picture. The mark tops out at “four or more”. A unary code of fifteen needs roughly 45pt of arc at the 2.0pt counter floor; the bottom opening is 12.65pt. It is not a design failure, it is the medium — and the menu behind the icon carries the exact number.

The short version. At 2× — every current Mac — everything in this mark clears its floor, most of it by 20–60%. At 1× the mark loses three things and keeps the rest: the counters between bands close, so the two arcs and the creature read as one nested mass; the three-unit tally fuses; and the sleeping eye disappears. In all three cases the information is carried redundantly somewhere that does survive. The one thing I will not claim is that the 0.32-alpha tracks render as anything but grey at 1×. They do not, and they were never asked to.
