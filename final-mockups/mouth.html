Mouth Gauge

Two open quota arcs; the bottom opening is a seven-socket register. Running agents fill it from the middle out, so the count is read from how closed the circle is — a shape change inside the existing silhouette, never a pixel outside it. Item box 22 × 22pt fixed, ink 16.00 × 16.00pt in every state.

The two 50% marks at 16×

Correction 4. Each mark is a disc whose diameter equals the stroke width of the ring it sits on — 1.50pt on the 1.50pt outer band, 1.25pt on the 1.25pt inner band — centred on the centreline at top dead centre. Its outermost point is tangent to the band's outer edge and its innermost point is tangent to the band's inner edge, so it protrudes in neither direction by any amount. The dashed rules below are those two edges; the disc touches both and crosses neither.

Two radii fall below the 1× floor by design: the crown valley (0.45pt) and the eye slot (0.225pt). Both fail toward roundness — the valley closes into a single dome, the slot closes into solid mass. Neither can produce a point. No vertex anywhere in the mark is unradiused.

The claim I will support: at 2× — every Mac shipped since 2012 — everything above resolves, including the exact agent count and the exact blocked count. At 1× the mark keeps its silhouette, its two quota readings, its alert and its sleep, and loses both numbers. I am not claiming 1× counting.

What the budget sacrifices: counter B (1.25pt) and the inner ring (1.25pt). Targeting exactly 16.00pt ink means 8.00pt of radius, not the 9.00 the brief allowed — a whole point less. I spent it on counter A (2.00pt, the only fully 1×-clean counter) so the two rings never fuse, and took it out of the inner ring and the creature’s breathing space. The creature is 3.80 × 3.30pt — small, and that is the cost.

The 50% mark, in full, because it is the tightest detail here. A notch is a counter, so it is on the 2.00pt floor. To leave 2.00pt of visible gap in a round-capped 1.50pt stroke the path must be cut by 2.00 + 1.50 = 3.50pt, which at r 7.25 is 27.7° — 11.5% of the 240° sweep, cut out of the one arc whose length is the number. A 1.00pt notch would clear none of this and would close up at 1× anyway. So: not a notch. A disc then has the problem the brief names: bigger than the stroke and it protrudes (forbidden); punched through and it is a counter again (2.00pt into a 1.50pt band would sever the ring). The way out is to stop solving it with geometry. The disc is exactly ⌀1.50pt on the 1.50pt band and ⌀1.25pt on the 1.25pt band — tangent to both edges, protruding nowhere — and it is separated from the ring by tone, not by a gap: the ring is knocked out under the disc and the disc is repainted at a fixed 0.65 alpha. That single constant is deliberately the midpoint between the lit arc (1.00) and the dim track (0.30), so the mark carries the same 0.35 alpha step whether the arc covers it (above 50%) or has already ended (below 50%) — a pinch in the first case, a bright dot in the second, and an event exactly at the boundary where the gauge most wants one. Alpha survives template-image conversion, and a tonal step has no minimum feature size the way a gap does. At 1× it degrades to a ~0.22 dip across the two device pixels either side of x = 11 — a soft grey pinch, not a crisp dot, and I am not claiming more than that. It cannot vanish and it cannot close up.

Pixel parity: ring edges sit at r = 8.00 / 6.50 / 4.50 / 3.25 / 2.00 from a centre on a whole point, so at 2× they land on 16 / 13 / 9 / 6.5 / 4 device px — four of five are whole. A circle can only be pixel-exact at four cardinal points anyway; the 1.50pt and 1.25pt strokes (half-point widths) are centred on the quarter-point radii 7.25 and 3.875 so their edges, not their centrelines, land on the grid.
