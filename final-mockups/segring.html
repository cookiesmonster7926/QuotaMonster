fm-segring

A 16.00 x 16.00pt menu-bar mark in a fixed 22pt box. Two open arcs show what is left of the 5-hour and the 7-day window; a creature in the middle moves only while agents run; the bottom opening counts them; and when a session needs you the whole ring closes, turns, and breaks into one segment per blocked session.

Live values in every render: 5-hour window 72% remaining - 7-day window 82% remaining - 4 sessions, 5 agents running, 1 session blocked for 2 minutes.
00

The four corrections

4 - the 50% mark is a circle, not a notch

A notch fails twice. Rasterisation: a gap cut into a stroke is a counter, so it needs 2.00pt of arc at 1x - 15.8° on the outer ring and 29.6° on the inner ring, 12% of a 250° sweep. Function: worse, when remaining is near 50% the arc's own round terminal arrives at top dead centre and the notch becomes indistinguishable from the end of the arc - the mark would fail exactly when it is needed. So: a filled disc of diameter equal to the stroke width, on the centreline. It is tangent to both band edges, so it protrudes nowhere. It never needs a counter because it is tonal: alpha 0.35 where it lies on live ink, alpha 1.00 where the arc has receded and it lies on the 0.28 track.

1 - no points anywhere

Every arc terminal is a round cap. The creature was redrawn as five arcs and two tangent line segments with zero vertices: crown lobes R 0.620, valley R 0.350 concave, body R 1.250. Chevron apexes are filleted before stroking so both the outer and the inner corner are arcs. Full audit in 02.

2 - the count never leaves the silhouette

The item box is 22 x 22pt in all fifteen states shown. The agent count lives in the bottom opening, on the outer ring's own centreline. Proof in 03.

3 - the ring divides by blocked count

N blocked sessions cut the alert ring into N equal segments separated by 2.00pt gaps, the first gap at bottom dead centre so the alert form echoes the resting one. One blocked session is a whole ring; the ceiling is six.

Reading order

The gauge runs clockwise from the left terminal round to the right one, and the agent pips carry on in the same direction: the first agent lights the pip nearest the right terminal.

One language, two rings

Both counters are the same idea - a ring cut into equal parts by 2.00pt gaps - applied to two stretches of the same circle at R 7.25. The blocked count takes the whole 360°; the agent count takes the 110° the resting arcs leave open. Nothing new is drawn for either.

The inner ring could not carry the agent count, and this is the honest reason. The method asked for the 7-day ring to be segmented by running agents. Its sweep is 17.58pt of arc at R 3.875. Five agents need four 2.00pt gaps - 8.00pt, 46% of the gauge - and the gauge still has to be read as a length. At 1x the only gap that survives is one that eats the quota reading. So the segmentation moved outward to the one stretch of ring that is already empty: the bottom opening. The conflict between count and fill is not solved on one stroke; it is avoided, on the same circle, in the same language.

Pixel parity. At top dead centre the outer band spans y 3.00 to 4.50: the outer edge lands on a whole device pixel at 1x, the inner edge on a half. A 1.50pt stroke cannot put both edges on a 1x boundary, so the outer edge was prioritised. The inner ring at 1.25pt spans 6.50 to 7.75 and is on a half-pixel at both ends at 1x and at 2x - it is soft at every scale. That is the price of holding both counters at exactly 2.00pt.
