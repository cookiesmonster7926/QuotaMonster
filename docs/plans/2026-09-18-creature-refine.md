# 生物圖示重畫 — 研究先行

## 為什麼上一版會醜（實證）

研究員抓了 14 個知名生物標誌的原始 SVG，自己在 16/22/32px 光柵化後量測；另一位直接在本機渲染 SF Symbols 量 alpha。三條關鍵發現：

1. **被公認「有設計感」的生物標誌，解法是把臉刪掉，不是把臉畫好。** 2012 Twitter 鳥是單一封閉路徑、零內部鏤空、沒有眼睛；GitHub mark-github octicon 同樣；Docker 單色資產沒有眼睛/嘴/鰭。
2. **兩隻眼睛在任何尺寸下都不行。** 同一顆頭在 22px 測了眼距 3/5/7/9/11/15%，六種全部讀成一張臉。縮小無效：3% 時得到一張臉外加兩粒灰塵。
3. **把生物畫成外框而非實體是最大殺手。** MySQL 13% 墨水/99% 糊、Tux 19%/81%、Postgres 20%/100%，16px 下全部不可辨識。活下來的落在 35–60% 墨水覆蓋率。

## 尺寸與筆畫的硬數字

- 畫布 22×22pt（`NSStatusBar.system.thickness` 實測 22.0）
- 目標 **16pt 實際墨水**，上下各留 ~3pt（Tailscale StatusBarIcon 與 SF Symbol `circle` 兩路交叉驗證）
- 單一筆畫粗細，落在標誌高度 **8–11%**（16pt 墨水 → 1.3–1.8pt）
- **筆畫下限 1.0pt**：0.40pt 在 1x 峰值 alpha 僅 0.49，沒有任何全黑像素
- 鏤空下限 2.0pt（1x）／1.0pt（2x）
- 複雜度預算 ≈ 4 條平行筆畫＝**一個剪影 + 最多一個修飾物**

### 輪廓優先 — The Ridgeback（63/80）

**立場：** Draw the outline first from three radii on a grid, refuse every interior detail that does not survive solid black at 22pt, and solve "needs input" as an obstruction in the world rather than an expression on a face.

**幾何建構：** Canvas 56 × 22pt, 1pt = 1px. Body ink 31 × 12pt (x 5–36, y 6.5–18.5); ground rail y 18.5–20.0 across the full 56pt.

Radius vocabulary is closed at three: R2, R5, R42. Two straight lines. Six tangent junctions, all G1.

Path: M5 16.5 → L5 11.5 (rear edge, 5pt vertical, tangent to both neighbours) → A5 about centre (10, 11.5) to the crown (10, 6.5), vertical tangent in, horizontal tangent out → A42 about centre (10, 48.5) to the hand-off (35.2, 14.9) → A2 about centre (34, 16.5) around the nose tip (36, 16.5) to (34, 18.5) → L7 18.5 (belly, 27pt horizontal) → A2 about centre (7, 16.5) back to start.

The load-bearing bit of geometry: the R2 snout circle at (34, 16.5) is internally tangent to the R42 back arc, because |O−N| = sqrt(24² + 32²) = 40 = 42 − 2 exactly. The hand-off point falls out of that: O + 42·(N−O)/40 = (35.2, 14.9). The back and the snout are one continuous curve, not two curves nudged together. The crown at (10, 6.5) is the shared horizontal tangent of R5 and R42; the belly is the shared horizontal tangent of R2-nose and R2-rear.

Stroke weight: one, 1.5pt, everywhere a stroke exists (rail, quills, wall) = 12.5% of the 12pt body, 6.8% of the 22pt canvas. Square terminals, butt caps, no rounded interior corners.

Quills: 1.5pt wide, 4.0pt drawn, pitch 3.5pt, so counter = 2.0pt exactly — the measured 1× counter floor. Six fixed slots, left edges at x = 7, 10.5, 14, 17.5, 21, 24.5; tops at y = 4, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5.5, 6.5, each embedded ~1pt into the body so there is no seam. Slots fill forward from the rump, so 1 agent puts its quill on the highest point of the back.

Fill: a rect x=5 w=31 clipped to the body, scaled in X. 1% = 0.31pt; shipped values snap to 0.5pt = 1.6% resolution. Three opacity plateaus only — 30% vessel and spent week, 60% burn projection, 100% eaten. No gradient anywhere.

Overrun dashes: 2.5 × 1.5pt at y 15.5, x 42 / 46.5 / 51, gaps 2.0pt, on the nose axis — the body's fill continuing past the snout.

Wall (needs input): 1.5pt wide at x 38.5, growing from the rail's own bottom edge (y=20) upward. 4.0 / 7.5 / 11.0pt for 1 / 2 / 3+ blocked. 2.5pt clear of the nose tip.

Optical corrections: (1) the mark is deliberately wide and short — 12pt of body in a 22pt bar against a ~8pt Wi-Fi fan and ~10.5pt battery — taking its weight from length the way Itsycal does, rather than filling the height like OrbStack; (2) the R42 back is far flatter than a "nice" arc would be, because a steeper back put the fill's leading edge below 3pt for the last quarter of the window; (3) quill tops sit a near-constant 2.7–3.0pt above the ridge rather than at one absolute height, so the spine reads parallel to the back; (4) at 10+ the shear is applied as skewX(−11°) *outside* scaleY, so the forward lean scales with quill height and the 2.0pt counters are preserved at the tips; (5) every horizontal and vertical edge lands on a whole or half point, so the whole mark is on the device grid at both 1× and 2×; the only curves are two arcs whose extrema are pinned.

**臉的問題：** The face is deleted outright — zero eyes, in every state, permanently. No eye, no eye-slit, no mouth, no front view, no morph. The research is unambiguous that there is no size at which a paired dot stops reading as a face, so there is no paired dot; and the Grazer's "closed eye-slit = zero agents" reading is carried instead by the smooth back, which was already doing that job.

Needs-input is re-cast from an expression to an obstruction: the ground rail turns up in front of the snout. It is the same 1.5pt weight, structurally continuous with the rail (it grows from the rail's own bottom edge), and it is the one place colour is allowed — amber. Blocked-session count is carried by height, not by repetition: 4pt / 7.5pt / 11pt for 1 / 2 / 3+. Nothing pairs, nothing is round, nothing sits above a curve, so no configuration of this mark resolves into a face.

Three things this buys beyond killing the smiley:
1. It costs one rectangle and destroys nothing. The old 220ms front-view morph obliterated all four other signals for the entire duration of the block — exactly when you most want to know your burn rate. The fill, the ghost, the quills and the rail all keep reading with the wall up.
2. It survives template rendering. Strip the colour and the wall is still a wall; the amber only raises it. The old state depended on amber eyes.
3. The blink survives the motion budget intact without animating a face: the 60-second blink becomes a 120ms alpha dip on the wall — same cadence, same hard stop at ten minutes — applied to the element that is actually asking for something.

The wait-time numeral is gone with the face. The numeral was the only element in the mark whose meaning changed between states, which is the critique's sixth flaw, and the honest fix at this size is deletion rather than relabelling: the exact figures live in the panel that drops down. Wait duration, if wanted, rides the wall's opacity tier (60% under a minute, 100% past it) without adding a shape.

**相對上一版改了什麼：** DELETED. The eye-slit. The front-view morph and the two-eye blocked state. The numeral (it was the only meaning-shifting element in the mark). Legs, feet, tail, ears, and the outlined contour. The spine arch at 10+ agents — it pushed the tallest quill to y = −1.0 on a canvas whose top is 0, so the lean is carried by a 0.5pt forward body shift and the 11° shear instead, and I would rather say that than ship ink off the canvas.

SILHOUETTE. The Grazer was a generic side-view quadruped with a gauge inside it. This is a rump-high, snout-low wedge: the gauge generates the form, and the animal is what the form happens to resolve into. Consequence that earns its keep — the dorsal ridge slopes, so the fill's leading edge is 12pt tall at the tail and 2.4pt at the snout. The last fifth of the 5-hour window is visually tense by construction, with no colour and no extra shape.

CURVE VOCABULARY. Closed at three radii (R2, R5, R42) with the snout circle internally tangent to the back arc at exactly 42 − 2 = 40. Rump radius went R6 → R5 in the second pass because at 12× the R6 rump read as a dome with a loaf under it; R5 lengthens the rear edge to 5pt, which reads as the flat cut end of a vessel rather than a haunch, and the R42 back is unaffected because the tangency constraint only involves the crown and the nose centre.

STROKE WEIGHT. One weight, 1.5pt, for rail, quills and wall. The brief's original ground — "a full-length hairline is the week, the heavy portion is what remains" — is two weights, which Apple and Material both forbid outright. Re-expressed as one weight at two opacities (30% spent / 100% remaining), which is Tailscale's exact two-plateau technique and also makes the stale state possible.

FILL COARSENESS (flaw 4). The old fill was a dark shape inside a dark body. Here it is a hard vertical edge between a 100% and a 30% plateau, which is the highest-contrast boundary the alpha channel permits, and it travels 0.31pt per percent: 28% and 34% are 1.86pt apart, about 4 device pixels at 2×, plus a 0.7pt difference in edge height. Snapped to 0.5pt, giving a real resolution of 1.6%.

BURN SEVERITY. Redefined from "how far past the snout the projection runs" to "how far before the reset you run dry" — 1 / 2 / 3 dashes at 15 / 45 / 75 minutes — because that is the quantity that decides whether you care. The real data (exhaustion 1h25m before the 3:40am reset) earns all three.

7-DAY. Doubly carried, which costs nothing: rail length and body height. When the week binds the ghost rail is deleted entirely so the rail visibly stops, a 1.5pt cliff stub drops below it, and the body scales to 0.82× about the baseline. Height above ground is the week's headroom.

QUILLS (flaw 5). Slots are fixed at 3.5pt pitch and fill from the rump forward, replacing the Grazer's continuous re-spacing — fixed slots keep every quill on the pixel grid, and the re-space transient becomes two clean 3.5pt translates. Terminals square, no taper, no diagonal.

WHAT I DID NOT FIX. Exact counts above six. Three legible tiers (1–6 exact, 7–9 at 1.25×, 10+ at 1.6× sheared) is what 1.5pt of ink plus a 2.0pt counter affords across a 19pt ridge, and pretending otherwise would be the sort of invented precision the critique was complaining about.

**套用的研究規則：** APPLIED, with the numbers they specify:
- 22pt working area, nothing taller. Idle ink 13.5pt (T6.5 B2.0), loaded 16.5pt (T3.5 B2.0), extreme 10+ state 18.8pt with 1.7pt clear at top.
- Organic forms must be silhouettes. Solid mass, distinctive outer contour, zero interior counters, zero interior lines. ~76% ink coverage of the body's own bounding box.
- Never two eyes; default to zero. Zero, in every state.
- One stroke weight (Apple HIG, Material). 1.5pt, no exceptions.
- Counter floor 2.0pt at 1×. Quill pitch is 1.5 + 2.0 = 3.5 exactly; dash gaps 2.0; nose-to-wall 2.5.
- Grid alignment beats everything. Every horizontal and vertical edge on a whole or half point.
- No diagonal strokes anywhere. The only non-orthogonal edges are the two arcs of a filled mass, never a stroked diagonal (Hidden Bar's 6%-solid chevron is the warning).
- Squared terminals, square interior corners.
- Opacity is the only shading: three flat plateaus (30/60/100), no gradient.
- Template-safe: the mark reads with colour entirely stripped; amber is decorative on the wall, not load-bearing.
- Asymmetric along the vertical axis, small head relative to body, closed radius vocabulary, anchors at extrema with axis-locked handles.
- Code-drawn rather than shipped as an asset, per Bjango's sanction for dynamic marks and Stats' precedent.
- Cover-the-head test: occlude the snout and the remainder still states "a vessel filling along a track."
- Motion: event-triggered only, zero idle motion, prefers-reduced-motion drops to static end-states that lose no information, no animation of any face because there is none.

DELIBERATELY BROKEN, and why:
1. Ink coverage 35–60%. The body is at ~76% of its own bounding box. That band was measured on square logo marks; a horizontal wedge that IS the gauge has to be solid mass or the fill boundary has nothing to bite into. Against the full 56×22 canvas the mark is 33%, and the three opacity plateaus keep its average optical weight below a solid system glyph until the hour is nearly spent.
2. Element budget "one silhouette plus at most one modifier." That arithmetic (16pt ÷ (1.5 + 2.0) ≈ 4 parallel strokes) was derived for a 22×22 square. This strip is 2.5 of those, and it spends the allowance on exactly three zones — creature, overrun, rail — with one element per 22pt of width.
3. "A circular item should be ~16×16pt." The mark is 12pt tall, not 16. It is not circular; it is a 31pt-long horizontal, and Itsycal's shipping 21×16pt slab is the right precedent for a wide mark. Matching 16pt of height here would have produced a black bar.
4. Bjango's "typically no padding." There is 3.5pt of top margin when loaded and 6.5pt when idle. That headroom is not padding — it is where the quills live, and the mark's height is itself a load signal.
5. The brief's "spine arched" at 7+ agents. Dropped: a 1.12× vertical scale on top of 1.6× quills put ink at y = −1.0. Stated rather than quietly fudged.

**評審必修：**
- (繪圖工藝, 30/40) Two problems, both at the front. First, the silhouette has no animal in it without quills, and the entry admits it — the fix is not more detail but proportion: the R5 rump against an R42 back is too close to a constant height, so give the rear 2pt more crown or drop the nose 1pt below the shoulder line and let the wedge actually taper. Second, the amber wall at x=38.5 and the first overrun dash at x=42 are two small rectangles 2.5pt apart, and in template rendering they are the same ink; that is a genuine collision in the one state that matters most. Move the dashes off the wall's band entirely (they sit at y 15.5–17.0, the wall runs to y=20) or make the wall full-height from rail to ink-top so it can never be counted as a fourth dash. Minor: the dash band centres on 16.25 against a nose axis of 16.5 — a 0.25pt step in the thing that is supposed to read as the fill continuing out of the snout.
- (產品訊號, 33/40) Urgency is a 1.5 x 4pt tick standing in empty space while the creature ignores it; give the animal a second drawn profile (nose up, not rotated) so the body itself reacts, and keep the wall for the count.

**自陳缺點：**
- Exact agent counts above six are unrecoverable from the mark. 7 and 9 are identical; 10 and 40 are identical. Three tiers is what the 2.0pt counter floor affords across the ridge, and I chose to say so rather than invent a fourth channel.
- The wall and the leftmost overrun dash sit 2.5pt apart and are both small rectangles. In monochrome (template rendering, or Reduce Transparency) a fast glance could read the wall as a fourth dash. Colour separates them in the one state where it is allowed, but the monochrome case is genuinely the weakest seam in the drawing.
- With the numeral deleted, there is no at-a-glance digit. A user who wants "what exactly is my percentage" has to open the panel. I think that is the right trade for a mark this size, but it is a real loss and some users will hate it.
- Idle, with the back smooth, the silhouette is closer to a wedge than to an animal. The creature reading depends on at least one quill being up. I show this honestly in the silhouette test rather than picking a loaded state for the hero shot.
- Burn-rate severity is quantised to three dashes, so the difference between exhausting 80 minutes early and 4 hours early is invisible. The ghost fill pinned at the snout says "bad" but not "how bad" beyond the third tick.
- The crouch and the 7-9 quill tier are implemented as vertical scales, which makes the radii anisotropic in those two states (R5 becomes 5 × 0.82 vertically when crouching). A shipped version should redraw those two profiles rather than scale them, exactly as Octicons redraws rather than interpolates.
- The 12pt body height is below the 16pt convention, so on a very busy menu bar the mark can read as slightly recessive next to Wi-Fi and the clock. That is deliberate — it is a background instrument — but it is a legibility cost on a light bar at 1×.
- Wait duration is encoded as a two-step opacity change on the wall, which is the weakest channel in the whole mark and will be missed by most people most of the time.

### 完全不要臉 — The Halt（60/80）

**立場：** I kept the idea and threw away the drawing: the creature is rebuilt as a constructed silhouette from three tangent circles on a groundline, with no face, no eyes and no interior line, so the body itself is the gauge and the only thing that ever moves is where the ink stops.

**幾何建構：** CANVAS 58x22pt. Ink box x2-56, y3.5-19.0 = 54x15.5pt (top margin 3.4, bottom 3.0, sides 2.0). ONE stroke weight everywhere: 1.5pt = 9.7% of ink height (SF Regular 8.3%, Octicons 9.4%). Every counter >=2.0pt.

RADIUS VOCABULARY - three circles, all tangent to the ground y=17.5: C1 rump (10.5,10.5) r7; C2 barrel (24.5,12.5) r5; C3 muzzle (38.5,14.5) r3. The dorsal ridge is the upper external tangent of C1 and C3 - computed, not eyeballed: shared unit normal n=(0.280,-0.960), tangency at (12.46,3.78) and (39.34,11.62), rake 16.3deg forward. C2 is then SOLVED rather than placed: it is the unique r5 circle tangent to both the ground and that dorsal tangent, which forces its centre to (24.5,12.5). The back is one straight rake, the belly datum one horizontal, and the middle circle is determined by the other two.

The belly is the only fourth radius: girth arc R40.45 centred (24.5,55.45), chord (10.5,17.5)-(38.5,17.5), sagitta 2.5pt. It lifts the barrel and leaves a 2.5pt counter (above the 2.0pt 1x floor), meeting the ground at a deliberate 20deg corner at each end - hind heel and lip contact. Those corners are what make it read as pressing its nose into the ground.

BODY PATH, one closed path, four segments, zero interior detail:
M38.5 17.5 A40.45 40.45 0 0 0 10.5 17.5 A7 7 0 1 1 12.46 3.78 L39.34 11.62 A3 3 0 0 1 38.5 17.5 Z

GROUND / 7-DAY: 1.5pt bar, x2-56, y17.5-19.0, same weight as everything else. Pale track at 0.26 = the week; heavy at 1.00 = what remains, squared terminal, no rounding.

FILL / 5-HOUR: three flat opacity steps only - 1.00 used, 0.56 projected, 0.26 empty. Built as a 0.26 hull plus two clipped rects at fill-opacity 1.00 and 0.405; 0.26 + 0.405*0.74 = 0.560 exactly. The fill edge travels x3.5 to 41.5, 38pt, so 1% = 0.38pt, and it is snapped to 0.5pt (about 1.3%) so the edge always lands on a device pixel at 2x.

QUILLS: six 1.5pt rects, pitch 3.5 (2.0pt counters), left edges 19 / 22.5 / 26 / 29.5 / 33 / 36.5, bases stepping 5.9, 6.9, 8.0, 9.0, 10.0, 11.0 down the rake. Each is 4.0pt tall with 1.5pt buried in the body so the sloped junction never opens a gap. Visible height 2.5pt; 7-9 agents = scaleY 1.15; 10+ = scaleY 1.375 (1.6x the visible 2.5pt) plus skewX -11deg. Butt terminals, vertical strokes, 100% solid at both scales.

BURN OVERRUN: 1.5pt horizontal dashes at 0.56, y13.5-15.0, x43.5 / 48 / 52.5, 2.5pt long, 2.0pt gaps - the ghost fill exiting the nose as a trajectory. 1-3 by dry-time before reset (under 30min = 1, under 90min = 2, 90min or more = 3).

HALT POSTS: 1.5pt vertical, 5pt tall, standing on the ground, x44 / 47.5 / 51, 2.0pt gaps, 100% (amber in the one colour state). Vertical + tall + opaque against horizontal + low + ghost: the two things that can appear in the forward zone can never be confused, and they are mutually exclusive by logic anyway.

OPTICAL: the mass sits left, so the forward zone carries the balance - geometric centring would drag the whole thing right. Left margin 2.0 and right 2.0 with the ground bar spanning full width is what keeps the item from reading as dead space. The rump circle is tangent to the ground at a single point; the 1.5pt ground bar absorbs that contact so it never looks pinched.

**臉的問題：** Killed outright. No eyes at any count, no front view, no morph, nothing bilateral, nothing that can be read head-on as a face. The needs-input state is solved as mechanics instead: THE ANIMAL STOPS EATING. The whole body rotates -9deg about the rump contact (10.5, 17.5) over 220ms, which lifts the muzzle 4.4pt clear of the ground - the one thing it does in no other state. The quills go rigid at a uniform 1.15, losing their stagger. The burn projection is deleted rather than dimmed, because a creature that has stopped eating has no burn rate: the ghost fill going to zero is physically true, not decorative. And one amber halt post rises on the ground ahead of the muzzle per blocked session - a wall it has stopped in front of. One post, two posts, three posts: countable vertical strokes, the most solid mark this drawing can make, and never a pair of anything in a face arrangement. Colour appears here and only here, and the state is fully legible in pure monochrome without it, because the lifted nose alone carries it.

The numeral ambiguity is fixed by invariance rather than by a better swap: the adjacent numeral is ALWAYS 5-hour percent used, in every state including blocked. The wait time is not in the mark at all - it belongs in the dropdown. One glyph, one meaning, forever.

**相對上一版改了什麼：** WHAT CHANGED FROM THE GRAZER, in craft terms.

DELETED: the front view and both eyes; every interior line; the numeral's second meaning; the closed eye-slit at zero agents; all colour except one state; the second stroke weight.

SILHOUETTE. The Grazer was an animal with a meter attached. This is a wedge whose mass IS the meter. It is now a single closed path of four segments with no counters except the belly arch - Twitter-bird and Docker-whale logic, where the form comes out of the construction and only incidentally resolves into a creature. Ink coverage is about 55% of the body bbox, inside the 35-60% band that separates marks which survive 16px from marks that smear.

PROPORTION. Body-dominant: r7 rump against an r3 muzzle, 2.33:1, with the head at the far end of a 38pt body and dropped to the floor. Big head on small body is the cartoonishness signal; this inverts it. The form is strongly asymmetric on the vertical axis, so bilateral symmetry can never suggest a face.

RADII. Closed vocabulary 7 / 5 / 3 plus one structural girth radius of 40.45. Three arcs and one straight line. C2's centre is solved from the other two rather than chosen, so rake, ground and barrel are one interlocked system instead of three placed shapes.

STROKE. A single 1.5pt weight across ground, quills, halt posts and dash height. The Grazer put a hairline groundline against heavier quills, which is the Ice mistake - two drawings stacked. The 7-day track is no longer a hairline; it is a 1.5pt bar carrying its state in opacity, which is the only shading a template image can keep.

COARSENESS. Two fixes. The fill edge now travels 38pt instead of the Grazer's short belly run, so 28% and 34% differ by 2.3pt = 4.6 device pixels at 2x, and the edge is snapped to 0.5pt. Second, and better: because the body tapers, the SEAM HEIGHT encodes position too - at 28% the fill edge is about 11pt tall down in the rump, at 80% it is about 4pt tall in the neck. You read x-position and edge-length at once. That falls out of the wedge; it is not bolted on.

QUILL SATURATION. Still six, but the three bands now differ in kind, not only in count: 1-6 is 1:1 at 2.5pt; 7-9 is six at 2.9pt; 10+ is six at 4.0pt sheared -11deg with the spine raked. A ten and a five can no longer be confused, and eight against twelve is a height and rake difference rather than an identical row.

MARGIN. Exactly two states break the 3pt margin: overload (quill tops to y1.9) and needs-input (to y2.13). That is deliberate - the mark gets physically larger only when it needs a human. Everything else sits at 15.5pt of ink inside 22, the Tailscale rhythm rather than the OrbStack puck.

GROUND SCALE. The 7-day track is whole above 25% remaining and only then becomes a linear gauge across the 14.5pt of runway ahead of the muzzle. An expanded scale in the danger zone: at 18% used the ground is simply whole and the mark is relaxed, where a linear map would have made it falsely tense.

CORNERS AND TERMINALS. Butt terminals everywhere, square interior corners, no rounding on anything 1.5pt wide. The two 20-degree belly corners are real corners, not fillets.

STALE. Everything to 34% with the heavy ground and the projection removed - Apple's disabled convention, and the loss of the projection is the honest part, since stale data has no forecast.

**套用的研究規則：** APPLIED: 22pt canvas, never taller (nothing exceeds y22). About 15.5pt of ink with 3.4 / 3.0 margins - the Tailscale 16pt-in-22pt rhythm. Single 1.5pt stroke weight at 9.7% of ink height, inside the measured 8-11% band, never below the 1.0pt floor and well under the 12.9% bold ceiling. All counters at or above 2.0pt (quill gaps 2.0, post gaps 2.0, dash gaps 2.0, belly arch 2.5). Every horizontal and vertical edge on a whole or half point so 1.5pt strokes land on whole device pixels at 2x. The organic form is drawn as a SILHOUETTE with solid fill and no interior detail; the single licensed counter is the belly arch. Three flat opacity steps (1.00 / 0.56 / 0.26) and no gradient, because a template keeps only alpha - 0.26 sits beside Tailscale's measured 30% and Apple's 35% disabled convention. Butt terminals, square interior corners, no rounding on 1.5pt shapes. Element budget respected: one silhouette plus one modifier row (the quills); the ground and the forward-zone marks are the instrument, not a second creature. Closed radius vocabulary of three circles plus one structural arc. Anchor points only at extrema, tangent continuity at every junction meant to read as smooth, actual corners where corners are meant. No triangles at all, so no centroid problem. Diagonals limited to the 16.3deg rake and the -11deg overload shear, both near-vertical or near-horizontal, so nothing is a 45deg smear. Template-safe: black and alpha only, legible on a light bar, a dark bar and a translucent wallpaper. Zero eyes - the finding that a 3%-15% eye sweep never stops reading as a face is taken literally and the entire channel is deleted.

DELIBERATELY BROKEN: (1) The 3pt margin, in exactly two states. Overload takes quill tops to y1.9 and needs-input to y2.13. Justification: growth is the only escalation channel left once the face is gone, it never exceeds the hard 22pt limit, and confining it to the two states that need a human makes the intrusion semantic rather than sloppy. **評審必修：**

**自陳缺點：**
- The blocked count is still a count of small repeated marks. Three halt posts at 22pt are countable; four would not be, and the design simply caps at three with no overflow signal.
- The -9deg rear is a 4.4pt lift. On a light menu bar at 1x over a busy wallpaper that is a real but not a loud change - it is the weakest of the five state channels, and it is carrying the most urgent state.
- The 7-9 agent band (2.9pt quills) against the 1-6 band (2.5pt) is a 0.4pt difference. At real size that middle band is close to invisible; only the 10+ band is unambiguous. The saturation problem is reduced, not solved.
- The belly arch drops to 2.25pt of clearance in the crouch, which is under the 2.0pt 1x floor once antialiasing is counted. On a non-Retina display the crouched creature may look like it is sitting flat on the ground bar.

### 幾何建構 — The Ruminant（54/80）

**立場：** Rebuild the animal from a declared vocabulary of two radii (2pt and 4pt) whose arcs may only begin and end at cardinal points of their own circles — a rule that forces horizontal or vertical tangents at every join, puts every anchor on a half-point, and leaves the back, belly and muzzle dead straight because straight edges are the only ones that rasterise black.

**幾何建構：** Canvas 66 × 22pt. Ink y 3.0 → 19.0 = exactly 16.00pt with 3.0/3.0 margins (Tailscale's measured figures). Modulus M = 2pt; the whole curve vocabulary is r = M and r = 2M. One r-4 circle builds the mass, three r-2 circles build the head.

Body path (grazing), 12 commands, every anchor on a 0.5pt multiple:
M17 16 · L17 12 (square tail) · A4→21 8 [C1 = (21,12), W→N] · L45 8 (dorsal ridge, 24pt straight) · A2→47 10 [C2 = (45,10), N→E, brow] · L47 12 (face, 2pt) · A2→49 14 [C3 = (49,12), W→S, cheek, concave] · L53 14 (muzzle, 4pt) · A2→55 16 [C4 = (53,16), N→E, snout] · L49 16 · L17 16 (belly, 32pt straight) · Z

Alert-low is the same 12 commands with the face segment collapsed to zero length (L47 10) and everything forward of it lifted 2pt. Alert-high additionally holds the underside at y 14 back to x 47. Identical command order across all three means Core Graphics interpolates them control-point for control-point over 220ms — the SF Symbols interpolatable-variant rule applied to a posture.

Stroke weight: 1.5pt everywhere (quills, ground bar, overrun ticks) = 9.4% of ink height, Octicons' exact figure. No second weight anywhere.

Counters: every counter in the mark is 2.0pt, the measured 1x floor — quill pitch 3.5 (1.5 bar + 2.0 gap), kerf slot 2.0 wide. Belly-to-ground gap is 1.5pt (Octicons' modifier gap).

Quill slots: six fixed positions at x 23, 26.5, 30, 33.5, 37, 40.5, short = y 5→8 (3.0pt), tall = y 3→8 (5.0pt). Fixed slots mean a quill can never land off the pixel grid, and the finish transient's re-spacing is a clean 3.5pt translation.

Ground: x 16 → 58 (42pt = one full week), y 17.5 → 19.0.
Numeral: right edge x 14, baseline y 16, cap height 7.4pt — deliberately under the menu bar's 9.16pt cap so it annotates the mark rather than competing with the system's own text.
Overrun ticks: x 57, 60.5, 64, y 10 → 14, rising off the muzzle line.

Optical correction: the mark is geometrically 16.00pt of ink centred 3/3, but its visual mass sits low (body plus ground occupy y 8–19). The tall quill was cut from 6.5pt to 5.0pt so the comb counterweights rather than overbalances the body — otherwise the mark reads as sitting high against the battery next to it. Fill levels use three flat alphas, 1.00 / 0.65 / 0.35, never a gradient.

**臉的問題：** Killed outright, exactly as the critic said. There are zero eyes in every state, including idle — the closed eye-slit is gone too, which also deletes the 60s blink and its 10-minute hard stop, so the mark now has no clock-driven animation at all.

The needs-input state is a POSTURE, not a face. A grazing animal that is interrupted lifts its head; if it is really bothered it comes up off its front feet. So the profile changes: the 2pt vertical face segment collapses to zero length and the muzzle rises 2pt (one blocked), and at two or more the underside also holds at y 14 back to x 47, opening 3.5pt of daylight under the chest. Both are the same 12-command path, so it is a 220ms path interpolation rather than a creature swap. It reads at 22pt because it is a silhouette change, and it reads with the template flag left on, in monochrome — the amber is reinforcement, not the signal.

The cost, stated honestly: the blocked COUNT is now a two-step ladder (one / more-than-one), not a counter. I accepted that. There is no channel left that could carry a number without adding a shape, and you go and look either way.

The numeral ambiguity is fixed the same way — by refusing to overload. The digits are always % of the 5-hour window used, in every state including blocked. The wait time moved to the popover.

**相對上一版改了什麼：** DELETED: the front view and both eyes; the idle eye-slit; the blink (and with it the last clock-driven motion); the numeral's second meaning; the arched spine on the 10+ state (the shear already said it).

REDRAWN, in craft terms:

Silhouette. The Grazer had a curved dorsal line. That curve was giving up most of its blackness to antialiasing before a single quill was drawn. The back is now 24pt dead straight at y 8.0, the belly 32pt straight at y 16.0, the muzzle 4pt straight at y 14.0. The tail end is a square vertical edge at x 17 with only the top corner rounded on r 4 — a capsule end read as a slipper; a square rump reads as a hindquarter.

Proportions. Body 38 × 8pt, a 4.75:1 horizontal mass. The head is 10pt of 38 and sits below the back line, reached by a small r-2 brow, a 2pt vertical face and a concave r-2 cheek. Small head, held low, is the strongest anti-cartoon lever available and it is the one Twitter pulled between 2010 and 2012.

The 5-hour fill. Was a sliding soft edge between two tones, which is why 28% and 34% were indistinguishable. It is now a 2.0 × 4.0pt kerf SLOT knocked clean out of the body at the boundary — a hole in a solid mass, the Itsycal technique. It is enclosed rather than a full cut, leaving 2pt bridges top and bottom, so the animal never breaks into two pieces and a quill always has solid ridge to stand on. The slot snaps to whole points, and you locate it against the silhouette's own landmarks: rump corner 11%, mid-back 50%, brow 74%, muzzle 84%.

The burn ghost. Still a pale band running ahead, but its far edge is now deliberately SOFT where the kerf is HARD. Hard edge = fact. Soft edge = forecast. Two boundaries, two grammars, never confused.

The quills. Six fixed slots instead of a free comb, so nothing lands off-grid and re-spacing is a 3.5pt translation. Two heights: six filled is the base, each tall one adds one more. 0–12 exact in the same width that used to saturate at 6. 13+ shears 11° forward and openly stops counting.

The ground. Same 1.5pt weight as the quills and ticks — the week is not a second gauge with its own weight, it is the same bar at a different alpha. One weight for the entire mark, no exceptions.

Optical corrections. The tall quill dropped 6.5 → 5.0pt so the comb does not overbalance the body upward. The untouched alpha was lifted from 0.30 to 0.35 so the head survives on a translucent bar. The belly-to-ground gap was opened from 1.0 to 1.5pt because at 1.0 the snout tip fused with the ground line at real size. In the crouch state the whole animal drops exactly that 1.5pt and closes it, belly onto ground.

Everything is drawn as mass. There is not one outlined organic form in the mark; measured ink coverage is 49% of the 46 × 16pt bounding box, inside the 35–60% band every legible creature mark in the reference set occupies.

**套用的研究規則：** APPLIED, with the specific numbers:
- 22pt working area; ink exactly 16.00pt with 3.0/3.0 margins (Tailscale's measurement).
- One stroke weight, 1.5pt = 9.4% of ink height (Octicons' figure, inside the 8–11% band).
- Every counter 2.0pt — the measured 1x floor, same as Tailscale's dot gaps.
- Every anchor on a 0.5pt multiple, every band edge on a whole point; snapped again at draw time.
- Organic form drawn as solid mass, zero interior lines, 49% ink coverage.
- Zero eyes. No paired dots at any size; no curve below anything that could pair.
- Asymmetric along the vertical axis; small head held below the back line.
- Closed radius vocabulary — two radii, four circles, declared before drawing.
- Arcs terminate only at N/S/E/W, tangents locked horizontal/vertical, continuity by construction.
- Square terminals throughout; no rounded stroke caps; no rounded interior corners.
- Three flat opacity steps (1.00 / 0.65 / 0.35) matching Tailscale's two plateaus plus Apple's ~35% disabled convention. No gradients.

DELIBERATELY BROKEN:
1. THREE opacity levels, where the research says 2–3 and warns about the third. Burn rate is the most actionable signal in the real data (exhaustion 1h25m before reset) and it earned a level of its own.
2. 66pt wide, not the ~16pt of ink a system glyph occupies. This is a gauge, not a glyph; the rubric's width rule is about height, which is obeyed exactly, but the optical-weight rule is stretched. I compensated by density rather than size — two thirds of the body sits at 0.65 or 0.35.
3. One shallow diagonal (about 4°) in the two alert postures. The rubric says budget diagonals knowing they read softer; this one is a filled edge, not a stroke, and it is the only non-orthogonal geometry in the drawing.
4. A numeral in the mark, which Apple permits only when essential. It is two digits, outside the silhouette, at a deliberately sub-cap-height size, and it now carries exactly one meaning.
5. Counters sit AT the 1x floor rather than above it, because 2.0pt gaps were the only way to get six quill slots onto a 24pt ridge. I would rather state this than quietly use 1.5.

**評審必修：**
- (繪圖工藝, 26/40) The kerf slot cannot exist where it is most needed. The slot is 4.0pt tall and needs 2pt bridges, so it requires 8pt of body — which the body has only back to x=45. At the brow landmark (74%, x=45.1) the body is 6pt tall, so the bridges are 1pt each; at the muzzle landmark (84%, x=48.9) the body is 4pt tall and a 4pt slot severs the head clean off, which is precisely the failure the entry says the enclosed slot was chosen to prevent. Above ~70% used — the danger zone the whole gauge exists for — the hard fill edge either breaks the animal or has to silently change to something else. Either taper the slot with the body (4.0 → 2.0 as it passes the brow, at the cost of your one-size rule) or stop the gauge at the brow and let the head read as overrun. Also fix the 4° claim, and reconsider the 2pt-tall snout: it is positive ink at the 1x floor, sitting 1.5pt off a 1.5pt rail.
- (產品訊號, 28/40) The head is 2pt tall and carries the urgent state; give the front of the body real height so the posture change is visible at 1x, and specify what the kerf does above 79%.

**自陳缺點：**
- Blocked sessions are a two-step ladder (one / more-than-one), not a count. Exactly what the brief asked to differentiate — needs-input with one blocked and with two — is carried by posture depth, not by a number, and beyond two it saturates immediately.
- Agent count saturates at 13+. Better than the Grazer's 7, but a fan-out of 40 looks identical to 14.
- 66pt wide is three system items' worth of menu bar. It is the single biggest thing a reviewer would push back on, and a 44pt variant would have to drop the numeral and two quill slots.
- Every counter sits exactly at the measured 1x floor of 2.0pt rather than above it, so on a non-Retina display the six-tall comb and the kerf slot are the first things to soften.

- The 7-day ground is read against the creature's own body rather than against an independent scale. One ruler serving two gauges is economical but needs explaining once.

- Amber in the needs-input state means that one image ships non-template, which is a real inconsistency in the asset pipeline even though the posture carries the state without it.

Three opacity plateaus only: 25% (empty vessel / spent week), 55% (burn ghost), 100% (solid). No gradients. Colour appears in exactly one state, amber #F0A72B, on one 2.5x4.5pt aperture and on 1-3 notches hanging below the ground line.

**臉的問題：** I kept the face and it survives, but only after three changes that the Grazer did not make. First, there is never more than one eye, in any state, ever. The blocked-session count is decoupled from the eye and moved to amber notches hanging below the ground line, which is a register the mark already uses for urgency (the 7-day cliff stub drops into the same band). One eye cannot configure into a face-pair, which is the whole finding from the eye-size sweep — shrinking a pair does not help, so I removed the pair. Second, the eye is not a dot. It is a 2x3.5pt vertical slot knocked out with evenodd, 2pt wide to clear the 1x counter floor, with 1.8pt of mass above and 2.5pt below. A vertical aperture in a profile head reads as a lens or a slit pupil — an instrument — where a circle reads as a googly eye. Closed, the same slot is fully covered: a 1pt residual line would fall under the counter floor and render grey, so closed means closed, and zero agents gives a completely smooth head and back. Third, and this is the real fix, the needs-input state is no longer a front view. The body stays in profile and only the head turns: the forehead rises, the crown rounds, the projecting snout foreshortens away (max x drops 58 to 56.6) and the jaw comes forward. It reads as an animal that has lifted its head and looked at you, it is never bilaterally symmetric, and it keeps the quota gauge fully readable while blocked — the Grazer's whole-body morph threw the gauge away at the exact moment you need to know whether you can afford to wait. The amber fills the existing aperture rather than adding a shape, and the monochrome fallback (Increase Contrast, template rendering) inverts the slot to solid ink, which still reads as a stare.

**相對上一版改了什麼：** Deleted from the Grazer: the front view, the two-eye state, the second stroke weight, all interior linework, and the numeral's second meaning. Added: a closed three-radius construction and a real tangent capsule. Specifically — (1) The body was a drawn creature; it is now a capsule struck between two R6 circles on their common tangents. The fill vessel is now the silhouette itself rather than an animal with a meter in it, and the top tangent is dead flat for 19pt, which is what lets six quills sit at uniform 3.5pt pitch without one of them standing on a curve. (2) Stroke weight is now a single 1.5pt for every ink element (9.4% of the 16pt ink band, the Octicons figure, inside the measured 8-11% band). The ground no longer uses a different weight to distinguish week-spent from week-remaining; that distinction moved to opacity, which is the only shading a template image can carry. (3) Three opacity plateaus, 25/55/100, replacing a continuous ghost. (4) Head shrunk to R4 with an R2 muzzle — 0.62 of body height. Head-dominance is the strongest cartoon signal and the Grazer's head was too big. (5) Interior counters cut from many to two, both 2pt wide: the eye slot and the girth slot. The girth slot is not decoration — it is the 50% keyline, placed at x=38 which is exactly half the 40pt gauge, so the fill edge always has a fixed landmark beside it. (6) The tail is the only asymmetry-generator at the rear and it is drawn as solid tapered mass off the R6 arc, not as a line. (7) Optical correction, one, declared: the mark is 2.5pt of margin at the bottom against 3.0 at the top. A ground-anchored form centred geometrically sits visibly high, because the eye reads the ground line as the base. The asset carries the correction as asymmetric padding so it can still be centred geometrically by AppKit. (8) Terminals are all square (butt) — quills, ground, ticks, cliff stub. Interior corners of both counters are square, not rounded, per Material. (9) The numeral moved to the left of the creature so the burn overrun ticks can spill off the right edge of the item, which is where "eating past its own nose" wants to point, and it saved 8pt of width. Total 66pt, the top of the ideal band. (10) Coverage measured on the silhouette test: roughly 44% ink of the creature's bounding box, inside the 35-60% band that separates marks that survive 16px from marks that smear.

**套用的研究規則：** Applied: 22pt canvas, never taller (only the 7-day cliff stub and the blocked notches cross below the ink band, deliberately). 16.5pt of ink with 3.0/2.5 margins, against Tailscale's measured 16.00pt of ink in a 22pt box. One stroke weight, 1.5pt = 9.4% of ink height (Octicons' exact figure; inside the 8-11% band; above the 1.0pt floor where strokes stop reaching black; below the bold ceiling). Counters at or above 2.0pt everywhere — quill gaps exactly 2.0, eye slot 2.0 wide, girth slot 2.0 wide — which is the measured 1x floor. Every edge on a whole or half point; 1.5pt elements on half-point centrelines, 2pt counters on whole points (parity rule). Organic form drawn as one solid mass, not an outline, with evenodd knockouts rather than drawn detail. Zero diagonals in the load-bearing geometry; the only sheared elements are the 10+ quills at 11 degrees, and they are short verticals that can afford to soften. Three flat opacity steps, no gradient. Template-safe: every state except needs-input is pure alpha. Square terminals and square interior corners throughout. Redrawn per size is honoured in principle — the gallery shows the same path at 1x and 3x, but the 1x row is where I judged it, and I would hand-fit a separate 1x bitmap before shipping. Deliberately broken, with reasons: (a) TWO knocked-out counters instead of the recommended one — the eye and the girth slot. The mark has to carry four independent signals, and two crisp 2pt counters cost less legibility than any alternative carrier I could find; both are fully enclosed by at least 1.8pt of mass. (b) The 10+ state pushes the quill tops to y=1.2, breaking the 3pt top margin. That state is supposed to feel like it is hitting the ceiling, and it is the only state where the mark is visibly taller than its neighbours. (c) Colour, in one state only, which the brief licenses and the template rule forbids — so that state ships as a non-template image with a monochrome fallback. (d) The severity ticks use 2.0pt gaps but sit only 1.5pt from the snout, tighter than the counter floor, because the overrun is meant to read as crowding.

**評審必修：**
- (繪圖工藝, 16/40) The tail is the worst-drawn element in the whole field. It is two freehand cubics closing at a 34.6° included angle, so its last ~2pt are under 1pt wide and the final half-point is a hairline that will not reach black at 1x; it is also claimed to come "off C1 at 225deg" from the R6 family when the tip (15.2,4.2) is 11.75pt from C1, not 6. Delete it — it carries no signal, it is the one purely decorative element in a mark whose whole argument is that nothing is decorative, and it is the single loudest cartoon cue left after the eye. Then either redraw the head on the circles you declared or stop declaring them. And the eye slot leaves only 1.7pt of ink between it and the crown in the side view and roughly 0.9pt in the turned variant — both under your own 2.0pt counter floor, so the eye will bleed through the top of the skull and turn the head into a hook.
- (產品訊號, 18/40) Delete the eye and the neck notch; a single vertical aperture inside a round head lobe is still a face at 22pt, as the 1x render shows.

**自陳缺點：**
- The 5-hour fill resolution is real but bounded. 1pt = 2.5%, edges snap to 0.5pt, so 28% and 34% differ by 2.4pt of hard vertical edge — visible against the girth keyline, but 28% and 30% are not distinguishable and no 22pt mark can make them so. I stopped pretending otherwise and put precision in the numeral instead.
- Agent count above six is a two-band texture, not a count. Six-at-medium means 7-9, six-tall-and-sheared means 10 or more. Eight and twelve now differ; eight and nine do not, and eleven and fourteen do not. The exact number belongs in the dropdown and I did not try to force it into the silhouette.
- Exact wait time while blocked is gone from the mark. Fixing the numeral's meaning to always be 5-hour percent used was the right call, but it cost the mark its ability to say two minutes versus twenty. The notches give three severity bands, not a duration.
- Two counters is over the measured element budget. If the mark had to survive a genuine 1x non-Retina bar with a quarter-point of phase error, the girth slot is the first thing I would cut, and the 50% keyline would go with it.
- The needs-input state is the only one carrying colour, which means it is the only one that cannot be a template image. It will not tint correctly when the menu-bar item is selected, and it will look wrong under Increase Contrast unless the monochrome fallback is wired up properly.
- The scenario here cross-dissolves between the side and turned silhouettes rather than interpolating the path. At 220ms and 22pt the difference is almost invisible, but the shipping version should interpolate point-for-point, which means both paths need identical node counts and I have not built them that way yet.
- The head turn is a smaller silhouette change than a full front view, which is the point, but it also means the needs-input state is less loud than the Grazer's. If a blocked agent is genuinely stopping all work, the amber is doing most of the shouting and amber is the one channel a colour-blind user or a template-forced render will not get.
- I have not hand-fitted 1x and 2x bitmaps. Everything here is one vector at both scales, which the research says is the thing that produces a mark that is wrong at both sizes.

## 完整研究規則

### 生物標誌工藝

The critic is right, and I can now show it empirically rather than assert it. I pulled the real SVG geometry for 14 well-known creature marks, rasterized them myself at 16/22/32px, and measured them. Two findings dominate.

First, the marks that everyone cites as "designed" solve the problem by DELETING the face, not by drawing it well. Verified from source files: the 2012 Twitter bird is ONE closed path with zero interior counters — no eye at all. GitHub's `mark-github` octicon is one closed path — no eye. Docker's own monochrome asset (I rendered and looked at it) has no eye, no mouth, no fin, no face of any kind: it is a lens-shaped hull, a tail, and nine rounded squares. The monochrome Firefox mark is a single subpath. Twitter deleted the bird's white eye in 2010 and never restored it. Meanwhile the creature marks that DO keep two eyes — Bun, Reddit Snoo, Android — read at 16px as exactly what your critic said: a smiley. My render of Bun at 16px is indistinguishable from a generic grinning blob.

Second, I ran a controlled test on the eye question because it is the load-bearing one for you. I drew the same generic creature head at 22px with the eye pair at 3%, 5%, 7%, 9%, 11% and 15% of mark width. Every single one reads as a face. There is NO size at which two paired dots stop triggering the face response — shrinking them does not buy seriousness, it buys grey dirt where eyes used to be. This matches the perception literature: a minimal three-dot triangular arrangement is sufficient for face perception, and the percept survives even when inverted. So "make the eyes smaller" is not a fix. The only fixes are one eye (profile, negative-space hole, 9–12% of width — what Deno does at a measured 11.4%) or zero eyes. Kill the front-facing two-eye state; the critic is correct and there is no compromise position.

The third finding is the one that will actually make your mark stop looking amateur, and it is not about eyes. I measured ink coverage and "grey mush share" at 16px across all 14 marks, and the split is absolute. Everything that survives is drawn as SOLID MASS: 35–60% ink coverage, under ~50% of its drawn area landing on ambiguous mid-grey (Twitter 49%/31%, Firefox 54%/34%, Evernote 53%/36%, Deno 41%/40%, Docker 39%/46%, Android 37%/40%). Everything that fails is drawn as OUTLINE: MySQL's dolphin 13% ink / 99% mush, Tux 19%/81%, the Postgres elephant 20%/100%, Mailchimp's Freddie 39%/68%. At 16px those four are literally illegible smudges in my renders. If QuotaMonster's creature has contours, whiskers, a drawn outline, or interior line detail, that is the actual reason it looks ugly — more than the eyes.

Finally, on "designed vs. mascot": the marks that read as instruments are ones whose FORM COMES FROM THE PRODUCT IDEA and only incidentally resolves into an animal. Docker is not "a whale" — it is cargo containers stacked on a hull. Twitter is not "a bird" — Twitter's own 2012 announcement described it as "crafted purely from three sets of overlapping circles — similar to how your networks, interests and ideas connect and intersect with peers and friends." For QuotaMonster this means the creature's body should BE the quota gauge — the silhouette itself should carry the fill level — not a generic animal with a meter bolted next to it. That is the difference between citing Stats/UTUVO and drawing a mascot.

Caveats on sourcing, stated honestly: secondary sources contradict each other on the Twitter bird's circle count (15, 14, and "two circles" all appear); I could not verify an exact number from a primary source, and only Twitter's own "three sets of overlapping circles" phrasing is firsthand. Apple's HIG pages are JS-rendered and I could not fetch them, so the menu-bar numbers come from Marc Edwards/Bjango and the animation presets from WWDC coverage. The Mozilla open-design blog does not actually discuss removing detail; the "didn't have to show a fox" quote reaches me via Wikipedia citing Tim Murray. Most SVGs I measured are Simple Icons' normalized monochrome redraws rather than official brand files — I verified Docker, Deno and GitHub against official sources directly.

**規則：**
- SILHOUETTE — Draw it as one solid black shape with zero interior detail first. If it is not identifiable that way, no interior detail will rescue it. Verified: the 2012 Twitter bird and GitHub's mark-github-16 are each ONE closed path with no interior counters whatsoever.
- SILHOUETTE — Hit 35–60% ink coverage of the bounding box at 16px. Measured on marks that survive: Twitter 49%, Firefox 54%, Evernote 53%, Deno 41%, Docker 39%, Android 37%. Every mark that failed my 16px render sat at 12–20% because it was drawn as outline, not mass (MySQL 13%, Tux 19%, Postgres 20%).
- SILHOUETTE — At 16px, under 50% of the drawn area may land on ambiguous mid-grey. Measured 'mush share': Twitter 31%, Firefox 34%, Evernote 36%, Deno 40%, Android 40% (all legible) versus Mailchimp 68%, Tux 81%, MySQL 99%, Postgres 100% (all illegible smudges).
- SILHOUETTE — No hairlines anywhere. Octicons mandates a consistent 1.5px stroke at BOTH 16px and 24px; nothing thinner than 1.5 device pixels may exist in the mark.
- SILHOUETTE — Every interior hole must be ≥1.5px at target size and fully enclosed by solid mass. Octicons: 1px gap to separate overlapping objects, 1.5px gap around modifier elements.
- EYES — Never two eyes, at any size. I tested the identical head at 22px with eye pairs at 3/5/7/9/11/15% of mark width: all six read as a face. The face percept never switches off; small eyes just become dirt. Backed by perception research — a minimal three-dot triangular configuration suffices for face perception and survives inversion.
- EYES — Default to zero eyes. The marks judged most 'designed' have none: 2012 Twitter bird, Docker whale (verified against Docker's own monochrome asset — no eye, mouth, or fin), GitHub Invertocat, monochrome Firefox. Twitter removed the white eye in 2010 and never brought it back.
- EYES — If you keep an eye, keep exactly ONE, in three-quarter or profile view, as a negative-space hole punched with fill-rule evenodd, sized 9–12% of mark width. Deno's official SVG measures 11.4%; my independent measurement of the Simple Icons redraw gives 11.2%.
- EYES — Any eye under ~6% of mark width must be deleted, not shrunk. MySQL's dolphin eye is 2.2% of mark width (0.35px at 16px) and Tux's pupils are 2.2% — both invisible, both leaving grey dirt.
- EYES — Never place any curve, arc or aperture below a pair of dots. Two dots plus a curve below is the emoji configuration and the brain resolves it pre-attentively.
- PROPORTION — Asymmetry along the vertical axis is mandatory. Every strong small creature mark is asymmetric: Twitter bird, Docker whale, Deno, Evernote, Firefox. Bilateral symmetry plus a head equals a face — Android and Snoo are symmetric, and both are faces on purpose.
- PROPORTION — Keep the head small relative to the body. Head-dominance is the single strongest cartoonishness signal. Twitter's team explicitly shrank the bird's head between the 2010 and 2012 versions.
- CONSTRUCTION — Fix a closed vocabulary of radii (three circle sizes is plenty) before drawing, and build every curve as an arc from that set. Grasser: "Once you agree on the construction method, that is really what's driving the shape... What gives the bird whatever charm it has is that it's constructed by that single shape."
- CONSTRUCTION — Anchor points only at N/S/E/W extrema, handles locked horizontal/vertical at those points, as few points as possible. Maintain tangent continuity at every junction meant to read as smooth; a deliberate corner must be an actual corner, not a tight fillet.
- CONSTRUCTION — Redraw per size; never scale one drawing. Verified: GitHub's mark-github-16 centers its circle at (7.984, 8.031) r=8 full-bleed, while mark-github-24 centers at (11.978, 12.545) r=11.5 inset. A 1.5x scale would put it at (11.976, 12.047) r=12. It does not. The 16px version also uses 7 curve commands to the 24px version's 5.
- CONSTRUCTION — Align outer edges of shapes to pixel boundaries; do not align inner edges. GitHub: "the font rendering engine tries to compensate. It does this by aliasing which renders the half pixels as grey pixels."
- CONSTRUCTION — Apply optical overshoot: round forms must exceed a matched square by roughly 1–3% to appear the same size.
- MENU BAR — The working area is fixed at 22pt tall and items cannot be taller. A circular item should be about 16x16pt to carry the same visual weight as system items. Ship as a template image (alpha channel only) so macOS tints it for light and dark automatically.
- MENU BAR — Check both polarities. The identical path looks heavier white-on-dark than black-on-light (irradiation); be prepared to ship a slightly lighter dark-mode variant.
- CHARACTER VS INSTRUMENT — The form must be derivable from the product idea, not from the animal. Docker is containers on a hull; Twitter described its bird as "crafted purely from three sets of overlapping circles — similar to how your networks, interests and ideas connect and intersect with peers and friends." For QuotaMonster the creature's body should BE the gauge, carrying the fill level in its own silhouette.
- CHARACTER VS INSTRUMENT — Run the cover-the-head test. Occlude the head; if the remaining form still states what the product does, it is an instrument. If it only says 'animal', it is a mascot.
- REDUCTION — Strip detail until the mark breaks, then restore exactly one step. Firefox removed the fox's legs entirely; Mozilla's Tim Murray said they "got a very clear signal that we didn't actually have to show a fox for people to know that it was Firefox." Twitter removed the feet, one wing feather and the head fluff.
- ANIMATION — Motion changes one property of the existing drawing; it never swaps in a different creature. Time Machine's menu-bar icon shows copying by rotating its existing clock face and ring.
- ANIMATION — Use the SF Symbols vocabulary macOS users already read: Rotate for in-progress, Pulse or Breathe for ongoing activity, Variable Color for a level changing over time (the natural choice for a quota), Wiggle for needs-attention.
- ANIMATION — Never animate the face. No blinking, no expression change. State belongs in the instrument part of the mark — fill level, rotation, opacity — never in emotion.
- ANIMATION — Opacity is a legitimate state channel; Apple uses roughly 35% opacity to indicate disabled menu-bar elements. Respect prefers-reduced-motion, and ensure the static frame carries the state unaided.

**反模式：**
- Two eyes, at any size. Verified across a 3%–15% sweep: the face reading never switches off.
- Eyes paired with a mouth curve below them — the literal emoji configuration.
- Eyes drawn as filled circles with a specular highlight dot.
- Shrinking the eyes to make the mark 'less cute'. Empirically this does not work; at 3% of mark width you get a face AND two specks of grey dirt.
- Drawing the creature as an outline instead of a mass. This is the single biggest killer: MySQL 13% ink/99% mush, Tux 19%/81%, Postgres 20%/100% — all illegible at 16px in my renders.
- Scaling one master drawing to every size instead of redrawing at 16 and 24.
- Off-grid coordinates that land on half pixels and render as grey fringing.
- Any detail under 1.5px at target size. Mailchimp's Freddie has features at 2.7% of mark width — 0.6px at 22px — and the whole mark collapses to a blob.
- Mascot logic: a full body with limbs, a pose, and an expression.
- Bilateral symmetry with a head at the top — this reads as a face no matter what you do to it.
- Big head on a small body.
- A generic animal with a product object bolted on beside it (a cat holding a gauge). The product idea must generate the form, not accessorize it.
- Gradients, drop shadows, inner highlights or multiple stroke weights. GitHub explicitly prohibits adding graphic effects to its mark.
- Post-rationalized construction grids — drawing freehand, then overlaying circles for the case-study slide. This is a widely named practice ('post-rationalisation') and experienced designers spot it instantly.
- Cartoon animation: blinking, bouncing, waving, squash-and-stretch.
- Evaluating the mark only at 512px. Every judgment must be made at 16px and 22px.
- Testing only in light mode, ignoring that the same path gains apparent weight when inverted white-on-dark.
- An unbounded curve vocabulary — too many distinct radii makes the mark read as hand-drawn rather than constructed.
- Whiskers, eyebrows, tongues, teeth, blush, or any other facial garnish.
- A mark that needs color to be identifiable.

### 選單列圖示工藝

A 22pt menu-bar mark is not a small logo — it is a piece of typography, and it is built the way a glyph is built: on a fixed grid, at one stroke weight, with counters sized to survive rasterization. Everything I could verify converges on the same numbers. macOS reports `NSStatusBar.system.thickness = 22.0pt` (measured on this machine, macOS 26.5), and Bjango's reference article states the working area "is fixed at 22pt, and items can not be taller" with "a good size for a circular item to feel the same weight as system menu bar items is 16×16pt." I confirmed that 16pt figure two independent ways: Tailscale's shipping `StatusBarIcon` is a 22×22pt template whose ink measures exactly 16.00×16.00pt with exactly 3.0pt margins on all four sides; and rendering Apple's own SF Symbol `circle` at pointSize 16 / scale Medium produces ink of exactly 16.00×16.00pt. So the target is: 16pt of ink, optically centered in a 22pt box.

Stroke weight converges just as tightly. I measured SF Symbols `circle` at pointSize 16 and found Regular = 1.33pt on 16pt of ink = 8.3% of the mark's height. Material Design specifies "a consistent stroke width of 2dp" on a 24dp grid — also 8.3%. Octicons uses 1.5 units on its 16 grid = 9.4%. The usable band is 8–11% of mark height, i.e. 1.3–1.8pt for a 16pt mark. Below that it dissolves: I rendered `circle` at ultraLight (0.40pt stroke) at 1x and it reached **peak alpha 0.49 with zero fully-opaque pixels** — a grey ghost, never black anywhere. Light (1.01pt) managed only 4 opaque pixels out of 102 touched.

The failure mode that actually makes a mark look amateur is rasterization phase, not taste. I measured a 12pt stroke in a 16pt box: at 1x, a 1.0pt stroke centered on a whole point peaks at alpha 0.50 — it renders as a grey line, not a black one. At 1.5pt it peaks at 0.75. Only at 2.0pt does it reach 1.00 and 100% solid pixels. A quarter-point of misalignment turns a black stroke grey and half-fills the counters next to it. Counters have a hard floor too: with a 0.25pt misalignment, a 1.0pt gap still reads 25% filled at 1x and only a 2.0pt gap comes back perfectly open; at 2x the floor is 1.0pt. Tailscale's dots are 4pt with exactly 2pt gaps — sitting precisely on that floor.

That arithmetic sets the complexity budget, and it is brutally small: 16pt of ink divided by (1.5pt stroke + 2pt counter) leaves room for about four parallel strokes. A 22pt mark carries one silhouette plus at most one modifier — not a scene. This matters most for the organic/biological forms in question, because curves and diagonals are the worst case: Hidden Bar's chevron has only **6% of its inked pixels at full black** (57 distinct alpha values) — it is a grey smear. Even Apple's own organic symbols are mostly antialiased at 1x (`ant` 7% opaque, `pawprint` 5%), which tells you Apple stopped hinting for 1x and relies on the silhouette carrying the meaning. So a creature mark must read as a filled silhouette, not as an outlined drawing with interior detail.

Caveats on what I could not verify: I had no access to Bartender, AlDente, CleanShot X, Shottr, MonitorControl, Mullvad or Dropbox assets, so I make no claims about them. My Docker finding is that the shipped asset has `isTemplate == false`; Docker could still set the flag at runtime, which I could not check. Apple's HIG does not publish a stroke-weight number for menu bar extras — the 8.3% figure is my measurement of SF Symbols, not an Apple statement.

**規則：**
- CANVAS: Draw on a 22×22pt canvas (verified: `NSStatusBar.system.thickness` returns 22.0pt). Nothing may exceed 22pt tall. A wide mark may be wider than 22pt, but never taller.
- INK SIZE: Target 16pt of actual ink inside that 22pt canvas, leaving ~3pt of margin top and bottom. Cross-verified: Tailscale's shipping StatusBarIcon measures ink of exactly 16.00×16.00pt with L3.0/T3.0/B3.0 margins, and SF Symbol `circle` at pointSize 16/scale Medium measures exactly 16.00×16.00pt of ink.
- STROKE WEIGHT: One weight for the whole mark, between 8% and 11% of the mark's height. For 16pt ink that is 1.3pt–1.8pt. Anchors: SF Symbols Regular = 1.33pt (8.3%), Material = 2dp on 24dp (8.3%), Octicons = 1.5 on 16 (9.4%).
- STROKE FLOOR: Never below 1.0pt. Measured at 1x, an SF Symbol `circle` at ultraLight (0.40pt stroke) peaks at alpha 0.49 with ZERO fully-opaque pixels; at Light (1.01pt) only 4 pixels of 102 reach full black. Sub-1pt strokes do not render as black — they render as grey.
- STROKE CEILING: Above ~13% of mark height the mark reads heavier than its menu-bar neighbours. Measured SF Symbols: bold = 2.06pt (12.9%), heavy = 2.44pt (15.3%), black = 2.90pt (18.1%) on 16pt ink. Stay at or below bold.
- ONE WEIGHT ONLY: Apple HIG, verbatim: "all interface icons in your app need to use a consistent size, level of detail, stroke thickness (or weight), and perspective." Material, verbatim: "System icons use a consistent stroke width of 2dp, including curves, angles, and both interior and exterior strokes." A second stroke weight inside one 22pt mark is always a mistake unless it is a deliberate optical correction (see next rule).
- OPTICAL CORRECTION IS THE ONLY LICENSED EXCEPTION: Material permits dropping to 1.5dp of the available 2dp "to fit multiple curves within the 24 x 24dp icon space" (their paperclip and smoking icons). That is a 75% reduction, applied only where curves crowd — never as a general lightening.
- COUNTER FLOOR (1x): Minimum 2.0pt of clear space between any two strokes if you care about non-Retina. Measured with a 0.25pt grid misalignment: a 1.0pt gap still reads 25% filled, a 1.5pt gap reads 25% filled, and only a 2.0pt gap comes back at alpha 0.00. Tailscale uses exactly 2pt gaps between its 4pt dots.
- COUNTER FLOOR (2x only): 1.0pt minimum. Measured: at 2x with 0.25pt misalignment a 0.5pt gap is 50% closed, a 1.0pt gap is fully open.
- ELEMENT BUDGET: 16pt of ink ÷ (1.5pt stroke + 2pt counter) ≈ 4. A 22pt mark can carry at most four parallel strokes across its width, which in practice means ONE silhouette plus AT MOST ONE modifier (a badge, a slash, a dot). Count the distinct closed shapes in your drawing; if it is more than 3, cut.
- PIXEL PARITY: A stroke's width parity must match its centerline parity. Measured at 1x: a 1.0pt stroke centered on a whole point peaks at alpha 0.50 (grey); a 1.5pt stroke peaks at 0.75; a 2.0pt stroke peaks at 1.00 with 100% solid pixels. Even widths want whole-number centerlines; odd/half widths want half-point centerlines.
- PIXEL PARITY AT 2x: A 1.5pt stroke (3 device pixels) centered on a whole point is only 50% solid. Shift it by 0.25pt so the 3 device pixels land whole. 1.0pt and 2.0pt strokes centered on whole points are already 100% solid at 2x.
- GRID ALIGNMENT BEATS EVERYTHING: With strokes exactly on the pixel grid (0.0pt offset), every width I tested rendered at alpha 1.00 with gaps at 0.00 — even a 0.5pt gap. With a 0.25pt offset, the same drawing loses 25% of its blackness and half-fills its counters. Snap every horizontal and vertical edge to whole device pixels before shipping.
- OPTICAL CENTERING, NOT GEOMETRIC: Apple HIG, verbatim: "Some icons — especially asymmetric ones — can look unbalanced when you center them geometrically instead of optically... Adjustments for optical centering are typically very small, but they can have a big impact on your app's appearance." Build the correction into the asset as padding so the asset itself can be geometrically centered.
- OPTICAL SCALE BETWEEN SHAPE FAMILIES: A circle must be larger than a square to carry equal weight. Bjango's formula: match the convex-hull AREA, which scales a circle to 112.84% of the square's side. Apple's own symbols land at 110%: at pointSize 16/Medium I measured `square` ink at 14.5pt and `circle` ink at 16.0pt. Use 110–113%.
- TRIANGLE CENTERING: Align a triangle by its CENTROID, not its bounding box. Bjango: "For triangles, the center of the bounding box often does not feel like the center of the triangle, and aligning by this method looks incorrect." This is the play-button problem.
- DIAGONALS: A 45° stroke conserves total ink but loses solidity. I measured identical 12pt strokes: at 2x a 1.0pt vertical is 100% solid pixels while the same diagonal is 18% solid. Budget diagonals knowing they will read softer, and prefer a filled silhouette over diagonal outlines for organic forms.
- ORGANIC FORMS MUST BE SILHOUETTES: Measured at 1x, Apple's own organic symbols are almost entirely antialiased — `ant` 7% fully-opaque, `pawprint` 5%, `gearshape` 8%. They survive on outline shape, not interior detail. For a creature/biological mark: solid fill, distinctive outer contour, at most one knocked-out counter. Delete every interior line.
- FILL vs OUTLINE: Apple HIG: "Symbols that use an enclosing shape — like a square or circle — can improve legibility at small sizes" and "The solid areas in a fill variant tend to give a symbol more visual emphasis." At 22pt prefer fill or a heavy enclosing form. Reserve pure outline for simple geometric marks with ≤3 strokes.
- KNOCKOUT COUNTERS ARE A LEGITIMATE TECHNIQUE: Itsycal's menu bar icon (21×16pt at 1x) is a solid rounded slab with the date reversed out of it. A filled slab with a knocked-out counter reads far better at 16pt than the same shape drawn as an outline.
- TEMPLATE IMAGE, ALWAYS: Apple HIG, verbatim: "Both interface icons and symbols use black and clear colors to define their shapes; the system can apply other colors to the black areas in each image so it looks good on both dark and light menu bars, and when your menu bar extra is selected." Set isTemplate / template-rendering-intent. Verify with a script that the shipped asset actually reports isTemplate == true.
- OPACITY IS THE ONLY SHADING ALLOWED: Since a template keeps only the alpha channel, use flat opacity steps for state. Bjango: "Apple typically uses 35% opacity to indicate disabled elements." Tailscale measured at two plateaus: 30% for inactive dots, 100% for active. Use 2–3 discrete opacity levels, never a gradient.
- ALPHA-LEVEL COUNT AS A CRISPNESS TEST: Count distinct alpha values in the rendered asset. Measured: Tailscale 12 levels (clean), Raycast tray-16 46, OrbStack 55, Ice IceCubeStroke 99 (smeary). A well-hinted small mark has few alpha levels because most of it is either fully on or fully off.
- SOLID-PIXEL RATIO TEST: Measure the fraction of inked pixels that render at full opacity at 2x. Measured: Raycast tray-16 74%, OrbStack 77%, Docker 65%, Ice 40%, Hidden Bar chevron 6%. Above ~60% is crisp; below ~40% is a grey smear. Run this on your drawing before shipping.
- ASSET FORMAT: Apple HIG: "If you create a custom interface icon, use a vector format like PDF or SVG." Ship vector plus hand-fitted 1x and 2x PNGs if you care about non-Retina. Bjango lists the valid options as "a single SVG, a single PDF, or a pair of PNGs (1× and 2× scales)."
- ONE SIZE PER ASSET, CONSISTENTLY: Every representation in the imageset must agree on the point size. OrbStack's MenuBarIcon ships three disagreeing sizes in one asset — 17×17pt at 1x, 16.5pt at 2x, and 16.2977pt in its SVG rep. Assert that all reps report identical NSImage.size.
- REDRAW, DON'T SCALE, ACROSS SIZES: Octicons draws every icon twice. Verified from raw SVG: `bell-16` builds its body from a radius-5 arc at (3,5) while `bell-24` uses radius-7 at (5,7); `gear-24` carries a separate inner-ring path (4pt outer / 2.5pt inner) that `gear-16` merges into one compound path. If you need a 16pt and a 22pt version, draw both.
- SHRINK PADDING AS THE MARK SHRINKS: Verified in Octicons: `three-bars-16` spans 13.75 of 16 units (86% of the box) while `three-bars-24` spans 17.25 of 24 (72%). Small marks need proportionally less breathing room, not more.
- KEYLINE GRID: Adopt Material's keyline discipline — a fixed set of shape anchors so unlike forms carry equal weight. On Material's 24dp grid: square 18×18dp, circle 20dp diameter, vertical rectangle 16×20dp, horizontal rectangle 20×16dp, all inside a 20×20dp live area with 2dp padding. Scale these to your 22pt canvas (roughly ×0.9) or use the 16pt live area from Material's dense 20dp grid.
- CORNER RADII: Material: "Corner radiuses are 2dp by default. Interior corners should be square, not rounded. For shapes 2dp wide or less, stroke corners shouldn't be rounded." Rounding the inside of a 1.5pt stroke at 22pt just eats the counter.
- TERMINALS: Material: "Use consistent stroke weights and squared stroke terminals" and explicitly "Don't use inconsistent stroke weights or rounded stroke terminals." Ice draws its chevron with `lineCapStyle = .butt` for the same reason. Round caps cost you roughly half a stroke-width of length at each end, which at 16pt is a visible amputation.
- MATCH ADJACENT TEXT: Apple HIG: "In general, match the weights of interface icons and adjacent text." The menu bar font is 13pt with 9.16pt cap height (measured via NSFont.menuBarFont). A 16pt mark sits deliberately above cap height — that is correct for the menu bar, but its stroke should still feel like the same weight as 13pt Regular SF.
- TEST WITH REDUCE TRANSPARENCY: Bjango flags this explicitly — the accessibility setting turns the menu bar flat dark or light grey, and "it's worth being aware that some people will see your menu bar extra with this option enabled." Also test on both a light and a dark menu bar, and in the selected (highlighted) state.
- TEST AT 1x EVEN IF YOU SHIP RETINA-ONLY: Render the mark at 1x and check that the silhouette still reads. Apple's own symbols are mostly antialiased at 1x (clear space 54–72%, fully-opaque 5–8%), so they survive on silhouette alone. If yours doesn't, the silhouette is too complex.
- SQUINT TEST WITH A REAL NEIGHBOUR: Place your mark next to the system Wi-Fi and Control Center items at actual size. Apple: "Depending on the visual weight of an icon, you may need to adjust its dimensions to ensure that it appears visually consistent with other icons." If yours is darker or larger, shrink the ink or lighten the stroke — do not adjust the canvas.

**反模式：**
- Scaling down a big logo. The app icon has shading, gradients and 5+ shapes; at 22pt it becomes a blob. The menu-bar mark is a separate drawing that shares only a silhouette idea with the app icon.
- Hairline strokes. Anything under 1pt never reaches black: measured, SF Symbols ultraLight (0.40pt) at 1x peaks at alpha 0.49 with zero fully-opaque pixels. It looks 'elegant' at 400% zoom and disappears in the menu bar.
- Ignoring the pixel grid. A quarter-point offset drops a 1.0pt stroke from alpha 1.00 to 0.75 and half-fills the counter beside it. This is the single biggest cause of 'my icon looks muddy' and it is entirely mechanical, not aesthetic.
- Too many ideas. A creature + a badge + a background shape + a slash is four shapes competing inside ~4 stroke-widths of space. The budget is one silhouette plus at most one modifier.
- Interior detail on an organic form. Eyes, legs, fur, veins, segment lines. At 16pt these collapse into grey noise — Apple's own `ant` and `pawprint` are only 5–7% fully-opaque at 1x and survive purely on outline.
- Counters under 2pt (1x) or 1pt (2x). The negative space inside a letterform, a loop, a gap between limbs — these close up first. Measured: a 1.0pt gap with 0.25pt misalignment is still 25% filled at 1x.
- Mixing stroke weights inside one mark. Apple and Material both forbid it in so many words. Ice's menu bar icon mixes a ~3pt hexagon ring with ~1pt interior cube edges and reads as two drawings stacked.
- A mark built entirely of diagonals. Measured: a 45° stroke renders only 18% solid pixels at 2x versus 100% for the same vertical. Hidden Bar's chevron is 6% solid with 57 distinct alpha values — a grey smear rather than a black mark.
- Shipping a non-template asset. Docker's `statusItemIcon-0` ships with isTemplate == false; a non-template mark will not invert for light/dark menu bars or highlight correctly when selected. (Docker may set the flag at runtime — I could not verify that — but the asset as shipped does not carry it.)
- Using colour in a template. Colour is discarded entirely; only the alpha survives. Any hue you draw is wasted work that makes the file look right in Figma and wrong on screen.
- Gradients or soft shadows for depth. A template keeps alpha only, so a gradient becomes a smear of partial opacity. Use 2–3 flat opacity steps instead.
- Filling the whole 22pt height. Measured, OrbStack's mark is 17×17pt ink with zero margins and 77% solid pixels — a black puck that shouts next to the system items. Ice's is 18×20pt ink with zero top/bottom margin. The system's own rhythm is ~16pt ink with breathing room.
- Inconsistent sizes across representations. OrbStack's single imageset declares 17pt at 1x, 16.5pt at 2x and 16.2977pt in its SVG. Non-integer sizes like 16.2977 are a tell that the artboard was never reconciled with the grid.
- Shipping only an @2x bitmap. Hidden Bar's ic_collapse provides a 32×32 asset at the 2x slot with nothing at 1x, so non-Retina users get a system downscale — the softest possible result.
- Rounded stroke terminals at small size. Material calls this out directly. A round cap eats half a stroke-width at each end; on a 16pt mark that is a visible shortening of every line.
- Rounding interior corners. Material: "Interior corners should be square, not rounded." Rounding inside a 1.5pt stroke consumes the counter you just fought to protect.
- Geometric centering of an asymmetric mark. Apple devotes a whole illustrated section to this. A downward arrow, a teardrop, a creature with a heavy tail — all sit visibly low when centered by bounding box.
- Bounding-box centering of a triangle. Use the centroid. Bjango demonstrates the bounding-box version looks visibly unbalanced when rotated.
- Matching a circle and a square at the same numeric size. The circle will look smaller. It needs ~110–113% (Bjango's area formula gives 112.84%; Apple's own symbols measure 110%).
- Text or numerals inside the mark. Apple permits it only "when it's essential for conveying meaning" and then requires localized variants. At 16pt, more than one or two characters is unreadable — and if you do it, do it as a knockout from a filled slab the way Itsycal does.
- Reusing one drawing for 16pt and 24pt. Octicons redraws every icon at both sizes with genuinely different path data and even different path counts. Interpolating one size to another produces a mark that is wrong at both.
- Keeping proportional padding constant as the mark shrinks. Small marks need proportionally LESS padding — Octicons' 16px icons fill 86% of their box where the 24px versions fill 72%.
- Never testing the real contexts: light menu bar, dark menu bar, selected/highlighted state, Reduce Transparency enabled, and 1x. Each one breaks a different class of mistake.
- Judging the drawing at 400% zoom. Every rule here is invisible at that magnification. Judge it at 1:1, in the menu bar, beside Wi-Fi and Control Center.

**實例：**
- **Apple HIG — The menu bar (Menu bar extras)** — https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar · It is the authority on the two non-negotiables: the mark is black-and-clear, and the system owns the colour. Verbatim: "Both interface icons and symbols use black and clear colors to define their shapes; the system can apply other colors to the black areas in each image so it looks good on both dark and light menu bars, and when your menu bar extra is selected. The menu bar's height is 24 pt." Note what it does NOT say: no stroke weight, no ink size, no grid. Apple leaves the actual craft undocumented, which is why the measurements below matter.
- **Apple HIG — Icons** — https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icons · The single most useful passage is on optical versus geometric centering, and it is worth following exactly: "Some icons — especially asymmetric ones — can look unbalanced when you center them geometrically instead of optically... Adjustments for optical centering are typically very small, but they can have a big impact on your app's appearance." Equally load-bearing is the consistency rule: "all interface icons in your app need to use a consistent size, level of detail, stroke thickness (or weight), and perspective. Depending on the visual weight of an icon, you may need to adjust its dimensions to ensure that it appears visually consistent with other icons."
- **SF Symbols — the construction system** — https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/creating-custom-symbol-images-for-your-app · This is the reference grid for anything that must sit next to system items. The scales are typographic, not arbitrary: "Each symbol is also available in three scales: small, medium (the default), and large. The scales are defined relative to the cap height of the San Francisco system font," and "Each of the nine symbol weights — from ultralight to black — corresponds to a weight of the San Francisco system font." For a custom symbol Apple asks you to match "level of detail, optical weight, alignment, position, and perspective."
- **Tailscale — StatusBarIcon** · It is the reference implementation of every rule at once, and it achieves state signalling without adding a single shape. Only 12 distinct alpha values in the entire rendered asset — almost every pixel is either fully on or fully off, which is what crispness actually looks like numerically. It signals connection state purely by opacity: I measured exactly two plateaus, ~30% for inactive dots and 100% for active, which matches Bjango's note that "Apple typically uses 35% opacity to indicate disabled elements." One shape type, repeated. No outline, no diagonal, no interior detail, nothing to dissolve.
- **Raycast — tray-16 and the '-16' icon system** · The system discipline is the lesson: every icon in the bundle is named with its grid size suffix (`tray-16`, `bar-chart-16`, `app-window-sidebar-left-16`), so the grid is enforced by the filename. The tray mark itself is clean — 74% of its inked pixels render at full black at 2x, one of the highest solidity scores I measured, because it is built from verticals, horizontals and generous arcs with no diagonals.
- **Itsycal — menubaricon** — https://github.com/sfsam/Itsycal · It solves the hardest small-size problem — carrying a numeral — by inverting the figure/ground. Instead of drawing an outlined calendar with a number inside it (which would need three strokes and two counters stacked in 16pt), it draws a solid filled slab and knocks the digits OUT of it as counters. The counters get the full weight of the surrounding black to define them, so a 2-3px numeral stroke still reads. Note also that it ships hand-made 1x and 2x bitmaps rather than one vector, which is the correct choice when you need pixel control at both scales.
- **Ice — IceCubeStroke** — https://github.com/jordanbaird/Ice · Worth stealing: its code-drawn chevron control item is exemplary — `lineCapStyle = .butt` (squared terminals, per Material's rule), `isTemplate = true`, and a clean 2pt line on a 12pt box (16.7%, deliberately heavy for a single-stroke mark that must read as a UI affordance). Worth avoiding: the ice-cube mark itself is 18×20pt of ink on a 20×20pt canvas with ZERO top and bottom margin, so it sits taller and heavier than its neighbours in a 22pt bar where the norm is ~16pt. And it mixes weights — a roughly 3pt hexagon ring against roughly 1pt interior cube edges — which Apple's consistency rule forbids outright.
- **OrbStack — MenuBarIcon** · Not good — included because the failure is precisely measurable. First, it is a black puck: 17×17pt of ink with zero margin on any edge and 77% of inked pixels at full black, so it is the darkest thing in the menu bar and pulls the eye away from the system items. Second, and more damaging, its three representations disagree about how big it is — 17×17pt at 1x, 16.5pt at 2x, and 16.2977pt in the SVG rep. A non-integer size like 16.2977 is a reliable tell that the artboard was never reconciled with a grid, and the mismatch guarantees the mark shifts between scale factors.
- **Docker Desktop — statusItemIcon-0** · Not good. The shipped asset reports isTemplate == false. The artwork itself is achromatic (I checked — zero chromatic pixel buckets), so the intent was clearly monochrome, but without the template flag the system cannot invert it for a light menu bar or tint it when the item is selected. It also tries to carry a whale silhouette AND a stack of containers on its back in 18×14pt of ink, which is well past the one-silhouette-plus-one-modifier budget. Honest caveat: Docker may set isTemplate programmatically at runtime, which I had no way to verify — my claim is only about the asset as shipped.
- **Hidden Bar — ic_collapse** — https://github.com/dwarvesf/hidden · Not good, and quantifiably so: only 6% of its inked pixels render at full black, across 57 distinct alpha values. It is a grey smear rather than a black mark, because a 45° stroke can never land on the pixel grid. Compare Ice's chevron, which solves the same problem in code with a butt-capped 2pt path. It also ships only an @2x bitmap with the 1x slot empty, so non-Retina users get a system downscale of an already-soft image — the worst case.
- **Stats — code-drawn menu bar widgets** — https://github.com/exelban/stats · The right architecture when the mark must be dynamic. Bjango explicitly sanctions it: assets "can also be drawn with code, if you require something dynamic, like an analogue clock or a calendar icon that shows the day of the month." Stats has no menu-bar entries in its asset catalog at all — I checked — because every widget is a draw(_:) override. Its constants also independently corroborate the 22pt figure.
- **Material Design — System icons** — https://m2.material.io/design/iconography/system-icons.html · It publishes the numbers Apple withholds, and they turn out to agree with Apple's practice. "System icons use a consistent stroke width of 2dp, including curves, angles, and both interior and exterior strokes" — 2dp on 24dp is 8.3% of the grid, which is exactly what I measured for SF Symbols Regular. It also names the parts, which is useful vocabulary for a critique: "stroke terminal, corner, counter area, stroke, counter stroke, bounding area." And it licenses the one exception cleanly: "If a system icon requires complex details, subtle adjustments can be made to improve its legibility. These adjustments are referred to as optical corrections... The paperclip icon uses 1.5dp of the possible 2dp stroke area to fit multiple curves within the 24 x 24dp icon space."
- **Octicons (GitHub Primer) — the dual-size grid** — https://github.com/primer/octicons · It is the cleanest public proof that you redraw rather than scale across sizes, and I verified it from the raw path data rather than taking the claim on faith. `bell-16` builds its body from a radius-5 arc starting at (3,5); `bell-24` uses radius-7 at (5,7) — different construction, not a transform. `gear-24` carries a separate inner-ring path (radius 4 outer, 2.5 inner) as its own <path> element, while `gear-16` collapses the same ring into a single compound path — the counter had to be simplified to survive at 16.
- **Bjango — Designing macOS menu bar extras (Marc Edwards)** — https://bjango.com/articles/designingmenubarextras/ · It supplies the two numbers everything else hangs on, and I independently confirmed both. Verbatim: "Despite the variation in possible menu bar heights, the working area for menu bar extras is fixed at 22pt, and items can not be taller. A good size for a circular item to feel the same weight as system menu bar items is 16×16pt. Assets can be a different size, provided they're not taller than 22pt." And: "There should typically not be any padding, unless it is needed to assist with vertical centering." Also the state-shading convention: "it's possible to use different levels of opacity to provide shading... Apple typically uses 35% opacity to indicate disabled elements."
- **Bjango — Formulas for optical adjustments (Marc Edwards)** — https://bjango.com/articles/opticaladjustments/ · It gives you a checkable number instead of a vibe, which is exactly what a rubric needs. On the circle-versus-square problem: "Rather than just scaling up the circle until you're feeling good vibes, what if the area of the square and circle matched? Doing this means the circle needs to be scaled by 112.84%." For irregular shapes it proposes convex-hull area — "It's like stretching a rubber band around the entire object" — so that holes and concave notches don't wrongly reduce the computed weight, which matters for creature silhouettes. On triangles: "For triangles, the center of the bounding box often does not feel like the center of the triangle, and aligning by this method looks incorrect" — align by centroid instead.
- **Dustin Curtis — Pixel-fitting** — https://dcurt.is/pixel-fitting · It names the mechanism behind the muddiness, which is the thing most people misdiagnose as a drawing problem. Verbatim: "After you resize a vector image onto a raster plane, you should zoom in to the pixel grid and manually refit the paths to pixel edges where it makes sense." And on why automatic scaling fails: "The computer has no way of knowing that the border should always be sharp, so it ignorantly tries to maintain the ratio of the originally-specified image at the expense of sharpness."
