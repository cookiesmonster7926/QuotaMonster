The Ruminant

A 66×22pt menu-bar mark for QuotaMonster. Three radii, one stroke weight, two counters, three opacity plateaus. The body is the 5-hour gauge; the ground is the week. One eye, never two.

1  Construction — 8×

Read it as a glyph, not a picture. Two R6 circles at (24,12) and (43,12) are joined on their common tangents at y=6 and y=18 — that capsule is the vessel, and the 19pt flat between the tangent points is exactly what six 1.5pt quills at 3.5pt pitch need (1.5 ink + 2.0 counter, the measured 1× floor). Head is R4 at (52,13), muzzle R2 at (56,14), snout tip x=58. Three radii, no fourth. Every edge lands on a whole or half point; 1.5pt elements sit on half-point centrelines so their three device pixels land whole at 2×. The girth counter is not decoration — centred on x=38 it is the 50% mark of the 40pt gauge, so the fill edge always has a landmark beside it.

2  Silhouette test

It passes, with one caveat I will not hide. As solid mass with every counter, quill and ground element removed, it still reads as a low grazing animal facing right: rump, dead-flat back, dropped head, blunt snout, tail hook. Ink coverage of its bounding box is roughly 44%, inside the 35–60% band that separates marks that survive 16px from marks that smear — and it is mass, not outline, which is the actual difference. The caveat: without the ground line under it the creature floats and the tail hook is the weakest passage, because it is the only place the drawing goes below 1.5pt of width. In shipping form the ground is always present, so it never appears alone like this.

3  In the bar — real size

Thu 2:15

Optical weight against the system glyphs. The battery and Wi-Fi beside it are drawn at 1.3–1.5pt on the same 22pt working area, and the Ruminant's 1.5pt is deliberately a hair heavier because it is mostly filled mass rather than outline — filled forms read lighter per unit of stroke than a ring does. The 25% empty vessel is what keeps it from becoming a black puck: only about a third of the mark is at full ink. On the light bar the same path gains apparent weight through irradiation, which is why the empty plateau sits at 25% rather than 35%; if it went higher the light-bar version would look muddy.

The numeral means one thing in every state: percent of the 5-hour window used. It never swaps to a wait time. That was the Grazer's worst bug — a digit that changes meaning is worse than no digit — and the cost of fixing it is that exact wait duration now lives in the dropdown, with the mark carrying only the three severity bands of the notches. Agent count above six is likewise a two-band texture (7–9 medium, 10+ tall and sheared) rather than a count, because six 1.5pt quills with 2pt counters is all 19pt of ridge can hold.

5  Live scenario — 16s loop

0.0–2.0Idle. Zero motion, aperture shut.
2.0One agent. Quill rises, 160ms.
3.0–3.3Fan-out to five, staggered 100ms.
3.2Fill steps 28→33%, 240ms.
6.0–6.7Agent done: flick 120ms, rise 5.2pt + fade 580ms.
6.7–6.88Spine re-spaces, 180ms.
8.0Session blocks: head turns, 220ms. Blink at 10.5s.
12.0–14.9Clears, agents finish, settle 260ms, then still.

Every animation here is event-triggered and terminates. The only clock-driven motion is the blink while blocked, once per 60s with a hard stop at 10 minutes (animation-iteration-count:10). After the settle at 14.9s nothing moves at all until the next event — the stillness is the completion signal. prefers-reduced-motion freezes every state to its static end frame with no information lost. One honest shortcut in this demo: the 220ms turn is a cross-dissolve between two silhouettes rather than a true path interpolation; shipping it means giving both paths identical node counts and tweening them in Core Graphics.
