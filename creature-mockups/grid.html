The Ruminant

A redrawing of the Grazer under one rule: every curve in the mark is a quarter-circle or semicircle of radius 2pt or 4pt, and every arc begins and ends at a cardinal point of its own circle. That single constraint forces horizontal or vertical tangents at every junction, puts every anchor on a half-point coordinate, and leaves the back, the belly and the muzzle as dead-straight lines. Nothing here is freehand. The face is gone.

1 · Construction

Modulus M = 2pt. The whole vocabulary is r = M and r = 2M — two radii, nothing else. Arcs terminate only at N/S/E/W of their own circle, so every tangent is horizontal or vertical and every anchor lands on a 0.5pt coordinate. The back (y 8.0), the muzzle (y 14.0) and the belly (y 16.0) are straight because straight edges rasterise at alpha 1.00 and 45° edges do not.

2 · The three postures — one path, interpolated

The blocked state is not a face. It is a posture. A grazing animal that is interrupted lifts its head; if it is really bothered it comes up off its front feet. All three outlines below are the same twelve path commands in the same order — M L A L A L A L A L L Z — so Core Graphics interpolates them control-point for control-point over 220ms. That is the SF Symbols rule for interpolatable variants, applied to a posture instead of a weight. The dashed outline in each frame is the grazing pose for reference.

3 · Silhouette test

Solid black, no interior detail, no opacity steps, no numeral. Ink coverage of the 46 × 16pt mark bounding box comes out at 49% by integration of the path — inside the 35–60% band that every legible creature mark in the reference set sits in. It reads: long flat back, rounded rump, muzzle down on the ground, quills up. It does not read as a face, because there is nothing on it that could be one.

6 · Live scenario · 18s loop

Idle → one agent → fan-out to five → one finishes → the spine re-spaces → a session blocks → it clears → everything winds down → the body settles once → still. Every timing below is the motion budget as specified. Nothing loops on a clock: the loop you are watching exists only because this page has to replay it.

7 · What changed from the Grazer, and why

Deleted
the front viewGone entirely. Two eyes at 22pt is a smiley at every size — the sweep from 3% to 15% of mark width never stops reading as a face. Offsetting them vertically does not help. The blocked state is now a posture.
the eye-slitThe idle "closed eye" is deleted too. Zero eyes in every state, so there is no face to animate.
the blinkKilled by the above. The mark now has no clock-driven animation at all — the 60s blink and its 10-minute hard stop are no longer needed, because there is nothing to blink. The motion budget went from "almost zero" to zero.
the dual-meaning numeralThe digits are now always % of the 5-hour window used. The wait time moved to the popover where it belongs. One glyph, one meaning.
the arched spineThe 10+ state used to add an arch on top of the shear. Two ideas for one signal; the arch is gone.
Redrawn
the whole bodyRebuilt from two radii (2pt, 4pt): one r 4 arc for the hindquarter, three r 2 arcs for the head, and five straight edges drawn with six line segments. Twelve path commands in total. Every arc runs cardinal-point to cardinal-point, so every tangent is horizontal or vertical and the joins are continuous by construction rather than by eye.
proportionsBody 38 × 8pt — a 4.75:1 horizontal mass. The head is 10pt of 38 and sits below the back line. A small, low head is the strongest anti-cartoon lever there is.
the back24pt dead straight at y 8.0. Straight edges rasterise at alpha 1.00; the Grazer's curved dorsal line was giving up most of its blackness to antialiasing before a single quill was drawn.
the 5-hour fillWas a sliding edge between two tones — 28% and 34% were 2pt apart on a soft boundary. Now there is a 2.0 × 4.0pt kerf slot knocked clean out of the body at the boundary. A hole in a solid mass is the most locatable feature there is — Itsycal carries a whole date that way — and the silhouette's own landmarks (the rump corner at 11%, mid-back at 50%, the brow at 74%, the muzzle at 84%) are the scale you read it against. It is enclosed rather than a full cut so the animal never breaks into two pieces, and so a quill always has 2pt of solid ridge to stand on.
the burn ghostStill a pale band ahead of the fill, but its far edge is deliberately soft where the kerf is hard. Hard edge = what has happened. Soft edge = what is forecast. You never confuse the two.
the quillsSix fixed slots on a 3.5pt pitch, so a quill can never land off the pixel grid. Two heights: 6 slots filled is the base, and each tall one adds one more. 0–12 exact instead of 0–6, in the same width. 13+ shears forward and admits it is not counting.
the groundSame 1.5pt weight as everything else — the week is not a second gauge with its own weight, it is the same bar at a different alpha. 1.00 remaining, 0.35 spent.
optical correctionThe mark is 16.00pt of ink with 3.0 / 3.0 margins, but the visual mass sits low (body + ground occupy y 8–19). The quills are the counterweight; the tall quill is 5.0pt rather than 6.5 so the comb does not out-weigh the body and pull the mark visually upward against the battery beside it.

8 · Implementation

Core Graphics

Three CGPaths built by addArc(center:radius:startAngle:endAngle:) from a table of four circles and six line segments. All three share the same command order, so the 220ms posture change is a point-for-point interpolation, not a cross-fade. Bands are clip(to:) the body path, then a handful of fill(rect) calls at 1.00 / 0.65 / 0.35 grey. The kerf is not drawn at all — it is the gap the rects leave. Every edge is snapped with round(x * scale) / scale before drawing.

Template & colour

image.isTemplate = true in every state but one, so AppKit derives the whole appearance from alpha and it inverts correctly on a light bar, a dark bar and when the item is selected. The only exception is needs-input, where the image is drawn non-template in amber. That state is still fully legible with the flag left on — the posture carries it — so the colour is a reinforcement, not the signal. Verify with a one-line script that isTemplate is what you think it is; Docker ships theirs false.

9 · Where it is still weak

Stated plainly, because the last version's problems were the ones nobody wrote down.

blocked countTwo steps only — one, and more-than-one. There is no channel left that can carry a number without adding a shape, and a count of humans-blocking is not something you act on differently at 1 than at 3. The popover has the list.
13+ agentsGenuinely saturates. Better than saturating at 7, but a fan-out of 40 looks the same as 14.
width66pt. That is three system items' worth of bar. Justifiable for a mark that is a gauge, not a glyph, but it is the single biggest thing a reviewer would push back on. A 44pt variant would have to drop the numeral and two quill slots.
1× countersEvery counter in the mark — quill gaps and the kerf slot — is exactly 2.0pt, which is the measured 1× floor, not a margin above it. On a non-Retina display the six-tall comb and the kerf are the first things to soften.
the brisket diagonalThe alert poses introduce one shallow diagonal (about 4°) that the grazing pose does not have. It is a filled edge rather than a stroke so it holds up, but it is the only non-orthogonal geometry in the whole drawing.
the head fadesThe fill runs tail→snout, so below about 80% used the head is the part sitting at 0.35 and the animal's most recognisable feature is its faintest. The silhouette test passes because it is drawn solid; the live mark at 28% leans on the quills and the flat back to carry recognition.
7-day resolutionThe ground is 42pt for 100% of a week, so one point is 2.4% — fine — but the creature stands on top of most of it, so you read the cliff against the animal's own body rather than against a scale. That is deliberate (one ruler, two gauges) and it is also the part most likely to need explaining once.
